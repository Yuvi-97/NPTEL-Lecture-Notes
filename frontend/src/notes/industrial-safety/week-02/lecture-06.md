# Week 2 – Lecture 6: Application of Hazard Identification Techniques

## 1. Key Concepts
- **System Breakdown Structure (SBS):** Deconstruction of a system into components and parts to understand sub-functions and functional relationships.
- **Hazard Triangle:** A model comprising three elements:
  - *Hazardous Element:* Hardware, energy source, or operational component (e.g., pressurized gas, electrical supply).
  - *Initiating Mechanism:* The triggering failure or condition (e.g., pump overrun, uninsulated wire, operator error).
  - *Target and Threat:* Vulnerable assets and potential harm (e.g., personnel fatality, fire, equipment damage).
- **Safety Ontology & Accident Path:** A structured network linking hazardous elements and initiating mechanisms to final consequences. 
- **Risk Control Systems:**
  - *Preventive Controls:* Break the initiating mechanism path before an accident occurs (e.g., maintenance, procedures, alarms).
  - *Mitigative Controls:* Reduce consequence severity after an accident occurs (e.g., emergency response arrangements).

## 2. Important Points
- **Pressure Tank Subsystems:**
  - *Storage System:* Pressure tank, pressure gauge, relief valve, discharge valve, alarm.
  - *Pumping / Supply System:* Pump, timer, electrical circuit (wiring, switch, power source).
- **Hazard Identification Tools Applied:**
  - **PHL & PHA:** Preliminary Hazard List catalogs hazards; Preliminary Hazard Analysis tracks the transition from Initial Mishap Risk Index (IMRI) to Final Mishap Risk Index (FMRI) via corrective actions.
  - **HAZOP:** Applied at discrete study nodes using parameters (e.g., flow, pressure) and guide words (e.g., *No*, *More*, *Less*, *Other than*, *High*, *Low*).
  - **FMEA:** Evaluates sub-component failure modes, causes, and local/system effects. Computes Risk Priority Number (RPN) using 10-point scales:
    $$\text{RPN} = \text{Probability (P)} \times \text{Severity (S)} \times \text{Detectability (D)}$$
- **Pareto Chart (80-20 Rule):**
  - Used to plot failure modes by RPN to distinguish the **vital few** from the **trivial many**.
  - In risk analysis, approximately 80% of risk is typically generated by 20% of failure modes.
- **Execution Requirements:** Hazard identification requires multi-disciplinary teamwork, engineering design knowledge, and historical operational data.

## 3. Differences / Examples
### HAZOP Nodes Example (Pressure Tank)
| Node / Location | Parameter | Guide Word | Cause | Consequence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pipe (Pump to Tank)** | Flow | *No Flow* | Closed inlet valve, pipe plug, pipe rupture | Gas not supplied |
| **Pipe (Pump to Tank)** | Flow | *More Flow* | Pump performance enhanced (high voltage) | High flow to tank |
| **Pipe (Pump to Tank)** | Flow | *Other Than* | Wrong material supplied | Air pumped instead of gas |
| **Pressure Tank** | Pressure | *High* | Pump overrun (timer failure) | Potential tank rupture |
| **Pressure Tank** | Pressure | *Low* | Gas inlet valve partially closed | Inability to pressurize |

## 4. Must Remember
- **FMEA Goal:** Lower overall RPN by systematically reducing $P$, $S$, and/or $D$ through maintenance and design modifications.
- **Pareto Principle Origin:** 18th-century observation by Vilfredo Pareto that 80% of world wealth was held by 20% of the population.
- **Other Advanced Hazard Techniques:** FMECA, Fault Tree Analysis (FTA), Event Tree Analysis (ETA), Hierarchical Task Analysis (HTA), Petri nets.
