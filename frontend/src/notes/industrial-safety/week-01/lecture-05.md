# Week 1 – Lecture 5: Safety Engineering & Accident causing mechanisms

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Safety Engineering:** A multidisciplinary domain focused on designing, monitoring, and controlling systems to protect people, property, and the environment.
- **Prevention through Design (PtD):** A lifecycle design approach where hazards across a system's lifespan are anticipated at the design stage and engineered out through upstream risk barriers and control design.
- **Bow-tie Tool:** Combines Fault Tree Analysis (FTA) and Event Tree Analysis (ETA); an effective tool for implementing PtD.
- **Safety Analytics:** Encompasses accident causation, prediction, and classification, along with identifying leading and lagging indicators for monitoring and control.
- **Negative Interactions:** Unintended or harmful interactions among the primary components of a system: human, machine, and environment.

## 2. Important Points
- **Common Features of High-Risk Plants (Kumamoto & Henley):**
  - Physical containment
  - Stabilization of unstable phenomena
  - Large size and complicated structure
  - New technology and variety of components
  - High inertia
  - Huge consequence potential and strict social demand for safety
- **Control and Protection Layers:**
  1. *Normal Control System:* Ensures intended operations/production occur within safe, designed operating limits.
  2. *Emergency Safety Systems:* Intervenes when system parameters breach normal limits (e.g., pressure alarms, relief valves).
  3. *Physical Containment:* Confines hazardous material or energy if barriers fail (e.g., underground nuclear waste storage, deep-mine fire sealing walls, flare stacks).
  4. *On-site Countermeasures:* Localized emergency response to protect workers and equipment at the accident location (e.g., fall retrieval systems, emergency medical response).
  5. *Off-site Countermeasures:* Protective measures deployed outside plant boundaries to safeguard the public and surrounding environment (e.g., perimeter gas detection, public evacuation plans).
- **Accident Analysis Framework (5W + 1H):**
  - **Why:** Focuses on the cause (root, direct, indirect, supplementary).
  - **How:** Emphasizes observational facts and sequence.
  - **What:** Identifies the specific negative interaction that took place.
  - **When:** Focuses on timing.
  - **Where:** Identifies location/place.
  - **Who:** Identifies responsible/involved personnel.

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Control Type | Pressure Tank Example Components | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Normal Control** | Timer, timer contact, manual switch, pump | Regulates normal gas transfer within design limits ($P_{min}$ to $P_{max}$). |
| **Emergency Control** | Pressure gauge, high-pressure alarm, relief valve | Prevents tank rupture if normal controls fail. |

## 4. Must Remember
- High-risk plant dynamics consist of **Challenges** (initiating hazards/mechanisms) vs. **Damages** (threats realized on targets).
- Negative interaction categories: unsafe acts (human harms self), abnormal plant states (unsafe conditions), abnormal states caused by human errors, human errors/injuries caused by the plant, and negative environmental influences.
- Accidents are predictable and preventable if system controls are designed and verified across all lifecycle stages.
