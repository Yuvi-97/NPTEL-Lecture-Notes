# Week 1 – Lecture 1: Introduction to Industrial Safety Engineering

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Industrial Safety Engineering**: A discipline focused on identifying hazards, assessing risks, and designing operational protections across a system's entire lifecycle (design, build, operation, maintenance, disposal).
- **Layers of Protection**: Hierarchical safety measures in high-risk plants: normal control systems, emergency safety systems, physical containment, and onsite/offsite countermeasures.
- **Petersen’s Accident Causation Model**: Attributes accidents to **system failure** and **human error**. Human error is driven by overload (capacity vs. load), traps (poor workstation design, incompatible displays/controls), and decision to err.
- **Prevention through Design (PtD)**: Anticipating hazards and designing engineering solutions, fail-safes, and mitigating measures before building and operating systems.

## 2. Important Points
- **Safety Knowledge Base Components**:
  - System concept and engineering details (e.g., Piping and Instrumentation Diagrams - P&ID).
  - Hazard identification, human error analysis, and software error evaluation.
  - Risk assessment and safety by design.
  - Supporting tools: safety analytics, data analysis, and management/performance assessment tools.
- **Multiple Stakeholder Responsibility**: Regulators, operational/maintenance personnel, management, and system design engineers share responsibility for industrial safety.

## 3. Differences / Examples
### Safety Issues vs. Health Issues
| Parameter | Safety Issues | Health Issues |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Time of Impact** | Immediate / short term | Prolonged / long term |
| **Example** | Struck by a falling object during material handling | Musculoskeletal disorders due to prolonged poor posture |

### Major Industrial Disasters Cited
- **Flixborough (1974)**: Ruptured temporary bypass pipe released 40 tonnes of cyclohexane (28 killed); caused by poor design by inexperienced engineers.
- **Bhopal Gas Tragedy (1984)**: Water ingress into MIC tank released 40,000 kg of MIC (2,500 immediate deaths, 8,000 subsequent deaths). Caused by disabled refrigeration, disconnected flare tower pipe, and turned-off gas scrubbers.
- **Chernobyl (1986)**: Core explosion during emergency core cooling test (31 immediate deaths); caused by operator errors, SOP violations, and lack of domain knowledge.
- **Piper Alpha (1988)**: Explosion and fire on an oil platform; 168 fatalities.

## 4. Must Remember
- Having safety systems designed into a plant is insufficient; they must remain operational, monitored, and maintained rather than bypassed to cut costs.
- Safety is not an intellectual exercise to keep people in work; it is a matter of life and death (Sir Brian Appleton).
