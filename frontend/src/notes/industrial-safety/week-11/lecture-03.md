# Week 11 – Lecture 3: Occupational Health & Safety Management Systems (OH&SMS) and OHSAS 18001-Part III

## 1. Key Concepts
- **World-Class Safety Maturity (Interdependent Culture):** The highest tier of organizational safety culture where safety is an internalized personal and collective value. Workers not only ensure their own safety but actively look out for, assist, and intervene to protect colleagues and contractors ("I take care of myself, and I take care of others").
- **The Four Safety Performance Archetypes:**
  - *Student A (Effort: Yes | Result: Yes):* Sincere, systematic effort yielding predictable, sustainable high-level safety performance.
  - *Student B (Effort: No | Result: Yes):* Fluke / luck-driven safety. No systematic effort is invested, but severe incidents have not occurred yet due to random chance (creates dangerous organizational complacency).
  - *Student C (Effort: Yes | Result: No / Delayed):* Genuine effort and resources are invested, but results take time to mature. Requires perseverance without abandoning systems.
  - *Student D (Effort: No | Result: No):* Total absence of safety effort, resulting in chronic accidents and regulatory non-compliance.
  - *Management Goal:* Cultivate and maintain an organization comprised strictly of **Student A** and **Student C** mindsets.
- **The K-W-S Competency Deployment Model:** A human resource safety development model aligning training according to three dimensions: **Knowledge ($K$)**, **Will / Commitment ($W$)**, and **Skill ($S$)** across organizational tiers.
- **Job Stress and Safety Performance Curve:** An inverted-U operational relationship: as workplace challenge and mental demands rise, performance increases up to an optimal ceiling. If demands exceed worker capabilities without skill enhancement, excessive cognitive stress triggers human error and sharp performance collapse.
- **"Show the Light" vs. "Show the Heat" Philosophy:**
  - *Show the Light (80%–90%):* Educating workers, communicating standards, explaining risk analysis results, and demonstrating how safe behaviors protect lives and families.
  - *Show the Heat (10%–20%):* Rigorous consequence management and disciplinary accountability for wilful violations of non-negotiable Life-Saving Rules.

## 2. Important Points

### 2.1 The Four Stages of Safety Maturity (Bradley / DuPont Framework)
1. **Level 1 — Reactive (Natural Instincts):**
   - Safety is driven purely by statutory policing or external inspector instructions. Workers comply only when someone watches or demands it.
2. **Level 2 — Dependent (Supervisory Policing):**
   - Safety rules exist, but compliance requires constant supervisor oversight and managerial guidance. Workers view safety as someone else's rulebook.
3. **Level 3 — Independent (Individual Competence):**
   - Workers possess high technical skill, personal discipline, and self-care. They follow procedures and take full responsibility for their own safety.
4. **Level 4 — Interdependent (World-Class Safety):**
   - Collective ownership and mutual care. Peer-to-peer coaching and courageous intervention are normative. If a colleague or contractor is at risk, anyone can halt the job without fear.

### 2.2 Case Study: Implementation in a 10-Million-Tonne Steel Plant (25,000 Workforce)
A global integrated steel enterprise with 25,000 employees achieved dramatic accident reductions by translating OHSAS 18001 into a unified operational framework:
- **Foundational Drivers:** Leadership investment of **Effort**, **Time**, and **Capital/Money**, engaging 100% of the workforce.
- **Core Corporate Values:** Integrity, Excellence, Unity, Responsibility (ownership of assigned tasks), and Pioneering.
- **17 Operational Guiding Methodologies:** Covering Leadership & Accountability, Competency Assurance, Three-Tier HIRA, Contractor Safety Management, Emergency Response, Prevention through Design (PtD), and Safety Analytics.
- **Emergency Response Standard:** Fire brigade and rescue teams must arrive at any plant emergency within **2 to 3 minutes**; fire response vehicles have absolute right-of-way with no plant speed restrictions.

### 2.3 Integration of Behavioral Safety and Process Safety
- **Behavioral Safety (80/20 Balance):** 80% focus on human behaviors, peer observation, coaching, and safety climate; supported by 20% baseline process understanding.
- **Process Safety (80/20 Balance):** 80% focus on deep engineering design, Process Hazard Analysis (PHA/HAZOP), containment reliability, and automated interlocks; supported by 20% operational behavioral discipline.
- *World-Class Synergy:* High-hazard facilities combine both models so that behavioral lapses do not defeat engineering barriers, and engineering safeguards protect workers during cognitive lapses.

### 2.4 K-W-S Model Distribution Across Corporate Hierarchy

| Management Tier | Primary Training Focus | Secondary Focus | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Frontline Workers & Supervisors** | **High Skill ($S$)** | Moderate Will ($W$) & Baseline Knowledge ($K$) | Hands-on execution of physical tasks, tool operation, and rapid emergency intervention. |
| **Middle Operations Managers** | **High Knowledge ($K$)** | High Will ($W$) & Moderate Skill ($S$) | Translating corporate safety policy into operational procedures, HAZOP reviews, and resource scheduling. |
| **Executive Leadership** | **High Will ($W$)** | High Strategic Knowledge ($K$) | Providing executive sponsorship, allocating capital budgets, and modeling uncompromised safety values. |

### 2.5 Practical Shop-Floor Safeguards and Life-Saving Rules
To eliminate major injury vectors, the enterprise implemented strict non-negotiable physical rules:
1. **Working at Height:** Mandatory 100% tie-off using dual lanyards attached to certified horizontal lifelines; eliminating working at height wherever possible via ground-level engineering.
2. **Railway Track Safety:** Barricading pedestrian pathways across internal plant railway networks, eliminating track-crossing accidents.
3. **Cylinder Handling:** Compressed gas cylinders stored strictly in an upright orientation, secured in certified transport trolleys.
4. **Three Points of Contact (Handrail Rule):** Mandatory holding of handrails whenever traversing plant stairways to arrest sudden slips or missteps.
5. **Ergonomic Handling:** Elimination of head-loading of industrial materials; mandatory deployment of wheelbarrows and mechanical lifters.

## 3. Differences / Examples

### 3.1 Organizational Safety Maturity Levels

| Dimension | Level 1: Reactive | Level 2: Dependent | Level 3: Independent | Level 4: Interdependent |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Worker Mindset** | "I follow rules only when inspected." | "I follow rules because my boss tells me." | "I follow rules because I care about myself." | "We watch out for and protect each other." |
| **Primary Driver** | Natural instincts / Fear of penalty | Supervisory control & policing | Personal knowledge & individual competence | Collective team ownership & mutual care |
| **Injury Rate** | High, recurring severe incidents | Moderate, plateaued rate | Low individual injury rate | Approaching zero; sustained world-class performance |

### 3.2 "Good Safety is Good Business" Empirical Evidence
In the 10-million-tonne steel plant case study:
- Divisions with the highest safety audit scores and lowest Lost Time Injury Frequency Rates (LTIFR) consistently achieved the highest operating efficiency, lowest equipment downtime, and superior profit margins.
- Operational discipline required to maintain zero leaks, pristine housekeeping, and strict procedural compliance directly translates to high product quality and process reliability.

## 4. Must Remember
- **Four Safety Maturity Levels:** Organizations evolve from **Reactive** (external policing) $\to$ **Dependent** (supervisory control) $\to$ **Independent** (self-care) $\to$ **Interdependent** (collective ownership: "I protect myself and my colleagues").
- **Core Enablers of Safety Culture:** Leadership must actively commit **Effort**, **Time**, and **Capital/Money** to empower the workforce; policies without resource backing fail.
- **K-W-S Model:** Frontline staff require **High Skill ($S$)**, Middle Managers require **High Knowledge ($K$)**, and Top Executives require **High Will ($W$)**.
- **Implementation Strategy:** Combine **"Show the Light"** (80–90% coaching, standards education, and positive motivation) with **"Show the Heat"** (10–20% strict consequence management for wilful non-compliance with Life-Saving Rules).
- **Good Safety is Good Business:** High safety performance and operational profitability are positively correlated; investing in risk control directly reduces downtime, scrap, and business disruption.
- **Inverted-U Job Stress Rule:** Cognitive overload leads to human error; increasing worker competencies expands the operational envelope before stress degrades safety performance.
