# Week 11 – Lecture 3: OH&SMS and OHSAS 18001-Part III

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Operational Safety Integrity:** The holistic condition of plant operations where technical assets, human operators, and operating systems reliably function within safe operating limits.
- **Three Pillars of Safety Integrity:**
  1. *Mechanical Integrity:* Reliability of equipment, pressure vessels, relief systems, and containment.
  2. *Operational Integrity:* Robustness of procedures, work permit systems, and process parameters.
  3. *Human Integrity:* Operator competence, fitness for duty, safety leadership, and disciplined execution.
- **Stakeholder Footprint:** Managing health and safety across the entire organizational boundary, including permanent employees, contract labor, visitors, and surrounding communities.

## 2. Important Points
- **Industrial Heavy Manufacturing Case Study:**
  - Steel manufacturing and metallurgical processing environments with extreme thermal and molten metal hazards.
  - Integration of contractor safety management: ensuring contract personnel receive identical safety inductions, task training, and personal protective equipment as permanent staff.
- **Management of Change (MOC):**
  - A formal system to review and approve all temporary or permanent modifications to equipment, instrumentation, raw materials, operating procedures, or staffing levels before implementation.
  - Prevents accidents resulting from unintended side-effects of engineering tweaks.
- **Community Safety Integration:** Facilities storing hazardous substances must coordinate emergency response plans with local fire departments, municipal authorities, and nearby schools/residents.

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Integrity Pillar | Focus Area | Verification Methods |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mechanical Integrity** | Physical hardware & vessels | Non-Destructive Testing (NDT), vibration monitoring, wall-thickness checks |
| **Operational Integrity** | Process procedures & workflows | Permit-to-work audits, SOP reviews, interlock trip testing |
| **Human Integrity** | People & safety culture | Competency testing, behavioral safety observations, toolbox talks |

## 4. Must Remember
- More than $60\%$ of major industrial incidents involve contractor personnel; contractor safety management is vital to overall plant risk control.
- Management of Change (MOC) is mandatory: unreviewed "minor field modifications" are a frequent root cause of industrial disasters.
