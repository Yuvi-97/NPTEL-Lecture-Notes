# Week 11 – Lecture 2: Occupational Health & Safety Management Systems (OH&SMS) and OHSAS 18001-Part II

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Occupational Health & Safety Management System (OH&SMS):** A structured, systematic framework comprising organizational structure, planning activities, responsibilities, practices, procedures, processes, and resources for developing, implementing, achieving, reviewing, and maintaining the occupational health and safety policy.
- **Specification vs. Standard:**
  - *Specification:* A detailed technical prescription of requirements that a product, service, or system must satisfy to be fit for purpose (analogous to standard clothing sizing for a 25,000-person workforce, eliminating ad-hoc variability).
  - *Standard:* A formally recognized, consensus-based norm adopted by international bodies after extensive operational trials and maturity.
- **OHSAS 18001 vs. OSHA:**
  - *OSHA (Occupational Safety and Health Administration):* A United States federal statutory agency under the Department of Labor that enforces mandatory national safety legislation.
  - *OHSAS 18001 (Occupational Health and Safety Assessment Series):* An international, voluntary management system specification applicable across any organization worldwide regardless of country or jurisdiction.
- **ISO 45001 (2018):** The international standard developed by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) that superseded OHSAS 18001:2007. It integrates OHSAS 18001 principles with International Labour Organization (ILO) guidelines and aligns with ISO 9001 (Quality) and ISO 14001 (Environment) via the Annex SL High-Level Structure.
- **Universal Applicability:** Neither OHSAS 18001 nor ISO 45001 dictates fixed, absolute numerical performance thresholds (e.g., a mandatory zero injury frequency rate). Instead, they provide a flexible, scalable specification applicable to any enterprise—from a 10-million-tonne integrated steel plant to a precision watch factory, a software IT campus, or a logistics network.

## 2. Important Points

### 2.1 The Economic and Human Rationale for an OH&SMS
- **Global Human Toll:** According to International Labour Organization (ILO) statistics, approximately $7,600$ workers die every day due to occupational accidents or work-related diseases, totaling over $2.7$ million fatalities annually.
- **Direct vs. Indirect Accident Costs (The Cost Iceberg):**
  - *Direct Costs:* Insured medical treatment expenses, workers' compensation payouts, and immediate hospital bills.
  - *Indirect Costs:* Uninsured losses, including legal fees, accident investigation hours, production downtime, plant cleanup, machinery repair, replacement worker training, diminished workforce morale, and corporate reputational damage.
  - *Cost Multiplier:* For every \$1 (or £1) of direct cost, indirect costs range between **8 to 36 times** higher depending on industry complexity and legal jurisdiction.

### 2.2 Historical Evolution and Limitations of Accident Causation Models
1. **Heinrich's Domino Theory and Safety Pyramid (1931):**
   - H.W. Heinrich analyzed insurance claims in simple, labor-intensive manufacturing environments and proposed the ratio:
     $$1 \text{ Major Injury / Fatality} : 29 \text{ Minor Injuries} : 300 \text{ No-Injury Incidents (Near-Misses)}$$
   - Modern extensions expand this base to $3,000$ near-misses and $30,000$ latent hazards/at-risk behaviors.
2. **Technological Phase Shift and Model Critique:**
   - In 1931, machinery was rudimentary, and safety focused predominantly on human worker behavior.
   - Modern capital-intensive industries (e.g., petrochemicals, offshore drilling, nuclear power) introduced high-energy technological hazards.
   - Catastrophic major accidents can occur without preceding minor personal injuries; relying solely on Heinrich's behavioral pyramid blinds management to severe technological and process integrity risks.

### 2.3 The PDCA Architecture of OHSAS 18001 / ISO 45001
The standard structures organizational safety governance into the continuous **Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA)** cycle:

```
          ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
          │               TOP MANAGEMENT            │
          │             OH&S POLICY COMMITMENT     │
          └───────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                              │
                              ▼
    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │                      1. PLAN                         │
    │  • 3-Tier Hazard Identification & Risk Assessment   │
    │  • Legal & Compliance Register                       │
    │  • Continual Improvement Objectives & Targets        │
    │  • Management of Change (MOC) Protocols              │
    └─────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                              │
                              ▼
    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │                       2. DO                          │
    │  • Resources, Roles, Competence, & Training          │
    │  • Communication, Worker Consultation & Engagement   │
    │  • Operational Controls (SOPs & Life-Saving Rules)   │
    │  • Emergency Preparedness and Response               │
    └─────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                              │
                              ▼
    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │                      3. CHECK                        │
    │  • Performance Monitoring & Proactive Inspections    │
    │  • Incident Investigation & Non-Conformance Review   │

    │  • Descriptive Data Analytics on Incident Logs       │
    └─────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                              │

    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │                       4. ACT                         │

    └─────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                              │
                              └───────────► (Continual Improvement Loop)
```

### 2.4 Detailed Operational Elements of the PDCA Cycle
1. **Plan:**
   - *Corporate Safety Policy:* Top executive commitment declaring that no injury or drop of blood is acceptable on the shop floor.
   - *Three-Tier HIRA (Hazard Identification and Risk Assessment):*
     - *Tier 1 (Workers):* Daily shop-floor Job Safety Analyses (JSA) and hazard reporting.
     - *Tier 2 (Supervisors / Officers):* Formal behavioral safety observations and workplace inspections.
     - *Tier 3 (Engineers / Specialists):* Formal Process Hazard Analysis (PHA, HAZOP, Fault Tree Analysis) on complex energetic systems.
   - *Management of Change (MOC):* Formal documented procedure to evaluate and mitigate risks before altering equipment, processes, raw materials, or organizational personnel.
2. **Do:**
   - *Competency and Training:* Ensuring operators possess certified skills before authorization.
   - *Operational Controls:* Physical interlocks, fail-safe mechanisms, standard operating procedures, and strict enforcement of **Life-Saving Rules** (non-negotiable safety rules where violations result in severe disciplinary consequence).
3. **Check:**
   - Periodic compliance evaluations, internal audits, and comprehensive accident/near-miss investigations.
   - Application of **Descriptive Analytics** to identify recurring patterns in incident timing, shifts, and equipment categories.
4. **Act (Management Review):**
   - Executive leadership evaluates whether the OH&SMS is achieving its objectives, compliance mandates, and continual improvement targets.
   - Integration of **Predictive and Prescriptive Analytics** to proactively target safety investments.

## 3. Differences / Examples

### 3.1 Standards Comparison: OHSAS 18001 vs. ISO 45001

| Feature | OHSAS 18001:2007 | ISO 45001:2018 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Origin & Nature** | Developed as a British / International Specification in 1999; revised to standard in 2007. | Developed by ISO in March 2018 as a universal global standard. |
| **High-Level Structure** | Standalone clause structure (Clauses 4.1 to 4.6). | Adopts Annex SL (10 Clauses), perfectly aligning with ISO 9001 and ISO 14001. |
| **Organizational Context** | Focuses predominantly on internal hazards within the plant boundary. | Explicitly evaluates external and internal context, interested parties, and supply chain. |
| **Leadership Emphasis** | Assigned responsibility to an appointed Management Representative. | Demands direct, non-delegable leadership commitment from top executives. |
| **Worker Participation** | Consultation on safety procedures. | Mandatory worker participation in hazard identification and OH&SMS governance. |
| **Scope of Risk** | Focuses on occupational hazards to worker safety. | Incorporates organizational risks, business continuity, and opportunities. |

### 3.2 Direct vs. Indirect Accident Cost Breakdown

| Cost Category | Direct Costs (Insured) | Indirect Costs (Uninsured) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Components** | • Emergency medical transport<br>• Hospital treatment fees<br>• Statutory workers' compensation | • Accident investigation man-hours<br>• Production line downtime & lost capacity<br>• Equipment repair / replacement<br>• Legal defense costs & fines<br>• Training replacement personnel<br>• Damaged reputation & lost business contracts |
| **Financial Ratio** | \$1 baseline | **\$8 to \$36** (800% to 3600% higher) |

## 4. Must Remember
- **OSHA vs. OHSAS:** OSHA is a mandatory US regulatory enforcement body; OHSAS 18001 (and ISO 45001) is an international voluntary management system specification applicable globally across all industry sizes.
- **Specification Evolution:** OHSAS 18001 originated in 1999 as a specification, matured into a full standard in 2007, and was superseded in March 2018 by ISO 45001 (aligning with ISO 9001 and ISO 14001 via Annex SL).
- **Indirect Cost Ratio:** Indirect (uninsured) accident costs are **8 to 36 times** higher than direct insured medical costs.
- **Three-Tier HIRA:** Effective hazard identification integrates worker-level JSA, supervisor behavioral observations, and engineer-led Process Hazard Analyses (PHA).
- **Management of Change (MOC):** Mandatory process requiring hazard evaluation prior to any modification in technology, equipment, process, or personnel.
- **Core Purpose of OHSAS / ISO Standards:** They do not prescribe absolute numerical injury rate targets; rather, they provide the structural PDCA specification to achieve systematic, continual improvement.
