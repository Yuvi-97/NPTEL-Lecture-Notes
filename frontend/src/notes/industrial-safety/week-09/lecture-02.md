# Week 9 – Lecture 2: Human Error, Classification and Causes (Contd.)

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Generic Error Modeling System (GEMS):** An integrated conceptual model developed by James Reason showing how humans switch between skill-based, rule-based, and knowledge-based cognitive modes when confronted with plant problems.
- **Brain Bottlenecks:** Neurological and cognitive constraints of human information processing (working memory limits, sensory overload, and attentional tunneling).
- **Violations:** Deliberate deviations from established rules, procedures, or codes of practice, distinct from unintentional errors.

## 2. Important Points
- **Sub-types of Mistakes (GEMS):**
  - **Rule-based Mistakes:** Misapplication of a good rule in the wrong context, or execution of an inherently bad/flawed procedure.
  - **Knowledge-based Mistakes:** Systematic cognitive errors arising during trial-and-error reasoning when no rules exist for a novel emergency.
- **Classification of Violations:**
  - *Routine Violations:* Common shortcuts tolerated by workplace culture to save time or effort.
  - *Situational Violations:* Caused by organizational pressures, inadequate tools, or bad weather.
  - *Exceptional Violations:* Rare, emergency deviations when adhering to protocol seems impossible.
- **Human Cognitive Bottlenecks:**
  - *Working Memory Capacity:* Limited to approximately $7 \pm 2$ discrete chunks of information (Miller's Law).
  - *Attentional Narrowing (Tunnel Vision):* Under extreme stress or noise, operators hyper-focus on one sensor/dial while ignoring critical alarms.

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Classification | Intentionality | Underlying Cause | Prevention Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Error (Slip/Mistake)** | Unintentional | Cognitive overload, bad interface, lapse | Ergonomic redesign, checklists, interlocks |
| **Violation** | Deliberate deviation | Inconvenient rules, time pressure, culture | Simplification of rules, enforcement, safety culture |

## 4. Must Remember
- Reference text: **James Reason (1990)**, *Human Error*.
- Errors reflect information processing limitations; violations are motivational, behavioral, and organizational problems.
- Operators under stress naturally fall back from knowledge-based thinking to familiar rule-based patterns, even when inappropriate.
