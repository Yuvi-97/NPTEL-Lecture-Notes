# Week 5 – Lecture 3: Energy Control Model and Hazard Control Hierarchy

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Energy Damage Model (William Haddon Jr.):** Theory stating that all industrial accidents and physical injuries result from the uncontrolled transfer or sudden release of energy (kinetic, thermal, electrical, chemical, or potential) across a boundary in amounts exceeding the damage threshold of human tissue or structures.
- **Hazard Control Hierarchy:** An internationally recognized descending order of effectiveness for mitigating workplace hazards, prioritizing design-based removal over administrative controls.
- **Prevention through Design (PtD):** The safety engineering philosophy of designing out hazards at the initial plant and equipment design phase rather than relying on retrofitted guards or PPE.

## 2. Important Points
- **Haddon’s 10 Energy Control Strategies (Core Principles):**
  1. Prevent the marshalling of energy.
  2. Reduce the amount of energy marshalled.
  3. Prevent the release of energy.
  4. Modify the rate or spatial distribution of released energy.
  5. Separate the energy in space or time from the vulnerable target.
  6. Place a physical barrier between energy and targets.
  7. Modify relevant basic surface contact characteristics.
  8. Strengthen the target's resistance against energy absorption.
  9. Rapidly detect and counter the damage.
  10. Provide post-event emergency medical treatment and rehabilitation.
- **The 5-Level Hazard Control Hierarchy (in order of decreasing effectiveness):**
  1. **Elimination:** Physically remove the hazard (most effective).
  2. **Substitution:** Replace hazardous material/machinery with a benign alternative.
  3. **Engineering Controls:** Isolate personnel from the hazard (barriers, ventilation, interlocks).
  4. **Administrative Controls:** Alter the way people work (standard operating procedures, job rotation, training).
  5. **PPE:** Protect the worker with personal protective gear (least effective, last line of defense).

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Control Level | Mechanism | Reliability | Practical Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Elimination / Substitution** | Eliminates hazard at source | Permanent / Inherent | Replace toxic solvent with water-based solvent |
| **Engineering Controls** | Isolates energy release physically | High (machine-based) | Acoustic enclosure around noisy compressor |
| **Administrative / PPE** | Relies on continuous human compliance | Low (error-prone) | Hearing protection ear muffs; 8-hr exposure limit signs |

## 4. Must Remember
- **Haddon's Model:** Hazard is fundamentally defined as an **energy source**.
- PPE is the **least effective** control tier because it does not remove the energy hazard and depends entirely on human behavior.
