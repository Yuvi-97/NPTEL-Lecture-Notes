# Week 5 – Lecture 4: Safety Function Deployment

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Safety Function Deployment (SFD):** A structured design methodology adapted from Quality Function Deployment (QFD) to systematically translate stakeholder safety needs and hazard control objectives into specific engineering design solutions.
- **House of Safety (HoS):** The matrix structure used in SFD that correlates "What" must be achieved (safety requirements and hazard mitigation goals) with "How" it can be accomplished (engineering design parameters and safety functions).
- **Inherent Safety Integration:** Embeds risk control principles directly into early-stage process equipment engineering rather than relying on bolt-on safeguards.

## 2. Important Points
- **SFD Implementation Steps:**
  1. **Identify Safety Demands ("What's"):** Gather critical hazard mitigation requirements derived from PHA, HAZOP, and accident history.
  2. **Assign Demand Weights:** Prioritize safety demands based on risk severity and regulatory mandates.
  3. **Establish Engineering Solutions ("How's"):** Formulate technical design features based on Haddon's energy model and the hazard control hierarchy.
  4. **Construct Relationship Matrix:** Score correlations between each "What" and "How" using standard weighting symbols (Strong = 9, Moderate = 3, Weak = 1).
  5. **Evaluate Roof Matrix (Interactions):** Identify synergistic or conflicting technical interactions between various safety functions.
  6. **Calculate Technical Importance:** Multiply demand weights by relationship scores to rank and prioritize safety interventions.
- **Industrial Case Application:** Illustrated using a **Desulphurizing Plant** in process metallurgy, translating gas leak and chemical explosion risks into automated isolation valves and inert purge systems.

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Aspect | Quality Function Deployment (QFD) | Safety Function Deployment (SFD) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Goal** | Customer satisfaction & product quality | Operator life safety & risk minimization |
| **Input ("What's")** | Voice of Customer (VOC) | Hazard scenarios, incident data, and safety standards |
| **Output ("How's")** | Product engineering specifications | Safety design interventions & physical barrier configurations |

## 4. Must Remember
- SFD prevents design conflicts where a proposed safety control inadvertently impairs operational functionality or introduces a secondary hazard.
- Engineering solutions derived via SFD should strictly follow the higher tiers of the hazard control hierarchy (elimination and engineering isolation).
