# Week 5 – Lecture 2: Consequence Assessment

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Consequence Assessment:** The quantitative estimation of potential physical damage, personal injuries, environmental destruction, and economic financial loss resulting from an accident scenario.
- **Overall Consequence Methodology:** Integrated industrial loss modeling framework developed by J. Maiti and N. S. Arunraj (*Journal of Hazardous Materials*, Vol. 169).
- **Physical Effect Modeling:** Uses mathematical formulations for gas/vapor dispersion, thermal radiation from pool/jet fires, and overpressure blast waves from vapor cloud explosions (VCE).

## 2. Important Points
- **Four Major Loss Categories:**
  1. **Human Loss ($L_H$):** Encompasses fatalities, permanent/temporary disabilities, hospital treatment expenses, and statutory compensation payouts.
  2. **Asset / Material Loss ($L_A$):** Direct replacement, repair, and reconstruction costs of damaged equipment, vessels, piping, and plant structures.
  3. **Environmental Loss ($L_E$):** Costs associated with toxic waste containment, soil/aquifer remediation, site ecological restoration, and regulatory environmental fines.
  4. **Business Interruption / Production Loss ($L_B$):** Losses incurred due to forced production downtime, delayed order penalties, lost customer goodwill, and market share reduction.
- **Total Consequence Formulation:** Total loss ($L_T$) is the aggregated monetary sum of all individual loss dimensions:
  $$L_T = L_H + L_A + L_E + L_B$$

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Loss Dimension | Direct vs. Indirect | Estimation Metrics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Human Loss** | Direct (medical) & Indirect (morale) | Probit equations (vulnerability to overpressure/toxic dose) |
| **Asset Loss** | Direct | Replacement equipment cost ($C_R$) and repair factor |
| **Environmental** | Direct cleanup & regulatory fines | Volume/area contaminated $\times$ remediation unit cost |
| **Business Downtime** | Indirect economic impact | Downtime hours $\times$ revenue loss/hour |

## 4. Must Remember
- Reference publication: **Maiti & Arunraj (2009)**, *Journal of Hazardous Materials* (Vol. 169, pp. 556–574).
- Consequence modeling translates physical explosion/toxic shockwaves into quantifiable monetary losses via vulnerability probit functions.
