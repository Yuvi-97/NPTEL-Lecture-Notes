# Week 5 – Lecture 1: Risk Assessment

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Risk Assessment:** The systematic process of identifying hazards, estimating the likelihood of undesirable events, and evaluating the potential severity of their consequences to guide safety decisions.
- **Risk Formulation:** Mathematically expressed as a function of incident frequency/likelihood ($L$) and consequence severity ($C$):
  $$\text{Risk} = \text{Likelihood} \times \text{Consequence}$$
- **ALARP Principle (As Low As Reasonably Practicable):** The regulatory principle dividing risk into three regions: Unacceptable region, Tolerable (ALARP) region where risk reduction must be implemented unless costs are grossly disproportionate to safety gains, and Broadly Acceptable region.

## 2. Important Points
- **Types of Risk Measures:**
  - **Individual Risk (IR):** The geographical frequency at which a specific individual is expected to sustain fatal harm at a given location per year ($10^{-5}$ to $10^{-6}$ per year criteria).
  - **Societal Risk ($F-N$ Curve):** Cumulative frequency ($F$) of accidents causing $N$ or more fatalities, assessing catastrophic group hazards.
- **Qualitative vs. Quantitative Risk Assessment (QRA):**
  - *Qualitative:* Employs risk matrices ($3 \times 3$ or $5 \times 5$) and expert ranking (e.g., PHA, HAZOP).
  - *Quantitative (QRA):* Uses numerical failure rates, probabilistic modeling (FTA/ETA), and dispersion/explosion consequence modeling.
- **ISO 31000 Risk Process:** Risk Identification $\rightarrow$ Risk Analysis $\rightarrow$ Risk Evaluation $\rightarrow$ Risk Treatment.

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Parameter | Individual Risk (IR) | Societal Risk ($F-N$ Curve) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Focus** | Risk to a single person at a specific coordinate | Collective risk to a surrounding population |
| **Representation** | Individual Risk Contours (iso-risk lines on maps) | Log-log plot of cumulative frequency ($F$) vs. fatalities ($N$) |
| **Regulatory Threshold** | Typically $1 \times 10^{-6}$ fatalities/year for public | Slope lines defining intolerable vs. acceptable societal limits |

## 4. Must Remember
- Risk assessment requires simultaneous quantification of both **probability** and **loss severity**.
- In the ALARP region, the burden of proof rests on the plant operator to show that further risk reduction is economically infeasible.
