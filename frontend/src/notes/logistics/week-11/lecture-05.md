# Week 11 – Lecture 5: Robust and Resilient Supply Chain Design

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Supply Chain Disruption:** High-impact, low-probability "Black Swan" events (e.g., pandemics, geopolitical wars, earthquakes, Suez Canal blockages, cyberattacks) that severely cripple logistics flows.
- **Robustness vs. Resilience:**
  - *Robustness:* The proactive ability of a supply chain to absorb and resist environmental shocks without experiencing functional performance degradation.
  - *Resilience:* The reactive and adaptive capacity of a supply chain to recover quickly, absorb damage, and restore equilibrium (or a superior operational state) after suffering a major operational breakdown.
- **Time to Recover (TTR):** The duration required for a disrupted supply chain node to restore full operational capacity.

## 2. Important Points
- **Resilience Engineering Design Strategies:**
  - **Multi-Sourcing & Near-Shoring:** Moving away from single-source offshore dependencies to regional dual-sourcing models (e.g., primary supplier + regional near-shore backup).
  - **Strategic Redundancy:** Holding strategic buffer inventories of critical components and maintaining warm backup manufacturing lines.
  - **Operational Flexibility:** Multi-skilled workforce, versatile modular tooling, and interchangeable component architectures.
  - **End-to-End Control Towers:** Centralized digital hubs monitoring global supply chain risk indices in real time.
- **The JD.com Case Study:**
  - High resilience built upon heavy direct investment in proprietary fulfillment infrastructure.
  - Complete elimination of multi-tier intermediate brokers; automated sorting and localized warehousing enabled rapid supply continuity during national transport lockdowns.

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Resilience Strategy | Mechanism | Cost Implication | Primary Benefit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Redundancy** | Holding buffer stock & excess capacity | Increases inventory holding cost | Immediate buffer against sudden stockouts |
| **Flexibility** | Multi-skilled labor & agile tooling | Upfront training & tooling investment | Rapid operational adaptation without excess stock |
| **Diversification** | Multi-sourcing across geographic regions | Lower volume discounts per vendor | Eliminates single-point-of-failure vulnerabilities |

## 4. Must Remember
- Lean, hyper-efficient single-source supply chains with zero buffer inventory are **extremely brittle** and collapse rapidly during unexpected global shocks.
- True resilience balances **cost efficiency during normal operations** with **built-in flexibility and strategic buffers** during systemic crises.
