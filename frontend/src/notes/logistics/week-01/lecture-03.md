# Week 1 – Lecture 3: Manufacturing Logistics and Industry Scenario

## 1. Key Concepts

- **Manufacturing Logistics:** Manages materials, resources, and processes involved in production, including procurement, inventory, scheduling, transportation, and distribution.
- **Manufacturing vs. Services Logistics:**
  - Manufacturing: Products are tangible and can be stored as inventory to meet future demand.
  - Services: Services are intangible and generally cannot be stored; capacity and customer queues must be managed.
- **Supply Chain Management:** Coordinates the movement of raw materials, subassemblies, and finished goods through suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, wholesalers, and retailers to customers.

## 2. Elements of Manufacturing Logistics

- **Inventory Management:** Maintains three main inventory types: raw materials, semi-finished goods, and finished goods. Balances holding costs with customer demand and safety-stock requirements to avoid stockouts.
- **Production Planning and Scheduling:** Answers five questions:
  - What to produce?
  - Where to produce?
  - How much to produce?
  - When to produce?
  - In what sequence to produce?
- **Outsourcing vs. In-house Production:** Outsourcing may be preferred when expertise, economies of scale, or investment are limited. In-house production is suitable when demand, technology, and expertise are available.
- **Quality Control:**
  - Incoming QC: Checks incoming materials and packaging.
  - Process QC: Monitors quality during production and identifies critical stages.
  - Outgoing QC: Inspects finished goods before dispatch according to the sampling plan.
- **Material Handling:** Movement and temporary storage of materials between production stages. Should be minimized to reduce damage and unnecessary handling.
- **Transportation:** Physically moves materials and finished products between suppliers, manufacturers, and customers.
- **Warehousing:** Provides storage, handling, and sometimes value-adding activities before distribution.
- **Reverse Logistics:** Handles returns, defective products, recycling, reuse, and remanufacturing; supports a closed-loop supply chain.

## 3. Factors Driving Logistics Development

- **Globalization:** Requires efficient transportation, distribution, and inventory systems to compete internationally.
- **E-commerce Growth:** Increases expectations for fast delivery, flexible fulfilment, multiple payment options, accurate tracking, and hassle-free returns.
- **Supply Chain Collaboration:** Information sharing among suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, and logistics providers improves coordination and decisions.
- **Advanced Technology:** GPS, RFID, cloud computing, and data analytics support tracking, demand analysis, and customized services.
- **Infrastructure Development:** Ports, airports, railways, and highways improve connectivity and logistics performance.
- **Emerging Markets:** Expansion requires understanding regional demand, cultural differences, climate, and local regulations.
- **Sustainability:** Green packaging, energy-efficient transportation, and renewable energy can reduce environmental impact.
- **Customer Expectations and Regulations:** Fast delivery, accurate tracking, environmental standards, and cross-border or interstate requirements influence logistics operations.

## 4. Global and Indian Logistics Industry

- **Global logistics market:** The lecture cites a value exceeding €8.4 trillion in 2021 and a projection above US$13.7 trillion by 2027.
- **Logistics costs:** The lecture cites approximately 10.7% of global GDP.
- **Third-Party Logistics (3PL):** External providers perform logistics activities such as transportation, warehousing, and distribution for manufacturers or sellers.
- **Indian logistics market:** The lecture cites approximately US$274 billion in 2022, with a projection of US$563 billion by 2030.
- **Indian logistics costs:** The lecture cites approximately 14% of GDP, compared with a BRICS average of 11%.
- **Transportation mix:** The lecture estimates that roads account for around 70–75% of Indian freight movement, increasing the importance of improving rail, waterways, and intermodal transport.

## 5. Major Challenges in Indian Logistics

- High order volumes and the need to prioritize urgent, perishable, and routine deliveries.
- Delayed payment cycles affecting cash flow between supply-chain participants.
- Roadblocks, accidents, weather disruptions, tolls, and checkpoints.
- Inadequate multimodal connectivity and port infrastructure.
- Shortage of trained and specialized personnel, drivers, and delivery staff.
- Slow adoption of technology due to high initial investment and training costs.
- Insufficient warehousing and cold-storage facilities, causing damage and losses.
- Taxation and regulatory differences that complicate transportation.
- Intense competition and increasing customer expectations for shorter lead times.
- High fuel costs that reduce profit margins.

## 6. Must Remember

- Manufacturing logistics focuses on **inventory, production planning, material handling, transportation, and warehousing**; services logistics focuses more on **queue and capacity management**.
- Inventory management must balance holding costs against stockout risks.
- The three quality-control stages are **incoming, process, and outgoing QC**.
- Reverse logistics includes returns, recycling, reuse, and remanufacturing.
- Digitalization, supply-chain collaboration, infrastructure, and multimodal transportation can improve logistics efficiency.
- Sustainable options such as recyclable packaging and electric vehicles can help reduce environmental impact and operating costs.
