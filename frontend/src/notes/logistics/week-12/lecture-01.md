# Week 12 – Lecture 1: Sustainable Supply Chain

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Sustainable Supply Chain Management (SSCM):** The strategic management of material, information, and financial capital flows across partner networks that simultaneously achieves goals across the **Triple Bottom Line (TBL)**: Economic, Environmental, and Social dimensions.
- **The Triple Bottom Line (John Elkington, 1994):** The three pillars of sustainability: **Profit** (economic viability), **Planet** (ecological integrity), and **People** (social responsibility and human welfare).
- **Scope 1, 2, and 3 Emissions:** Standard greenhouse gas accounting categories covering direct company-owned emissions (Scope 1), purchased electricity/energy emissions (Scope 2), and broad indirect upstream/downstream supply chain emissions (Scope 3, often accounting for $> 80\%$ of total corporate carbon footprint).

## 2. Important Points
- **The Three Pillars of SSCM Detailed:**
  1. **Economic Pillar:** Ensuring long-term enterprise profitability, risk mitigation, and cost efficiency achieved through lean waste elimination and energy conservation.
  2. **Environmental Pillar:** Decarbonization of transport fleets, renewable energy adoption in warehouses (solar panels), eco-friendly packaging, toxic chemical elimination, and water stewardship.
  3. **Social Pillar:** Fair living wages, eradication of child/forced labor in developing-nation supplier tiers, safe working conditions, worker health insurance, and local community development.
- **Drivers of Supply Chain Sustainability:**
  - Tightening government regulations (carbon border taxes, mandatory ESG reporting).
  - Ethical consumer buying preferences demanding sustainable and cruelty-free products.
  - Institutional investor pressure linking capital financing to high ESG performance ratings.

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Sustainability Pillar | Operational Focus | Key Supply Chain Metric |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Economic** | Financial viability & cost control | Return on Investment (ROI), total landed logistics cost |
| **Environmental** | Ecological protection & emissions | Carbon intensity ($g CO_2e / \text{ton-km}$), landfill diversion $\%$ |
| **Social** | Human rights & worker welfare | Lost Time Injury Frequency Rate (LTIFR), fair wage audit compliance |

## 4. Must Remember
- Reference framework: **John Elkington (1994)** coined the "Triple Bottom Line" concept.
- True sustainability cannot focus exclusively on environmental ecology while ignoring financial viability or human labor conditions: **all three pillars must be balanced simultaneously**.
