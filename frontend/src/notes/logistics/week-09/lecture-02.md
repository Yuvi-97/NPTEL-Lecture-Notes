# Week 9 – Lecture 2: Designing Distribution Networks in Supply Chain

## 1. Key Concepts

- **Distribution Network Design Archetypes:** The six fundamental structural configurations governing how inventory is physically stored, routed, and handed over from upstream manufacturers to downstream consumers.
- **Drop-Shipping (Manufacturer Storage with Direct Shipping):** A fulfillment model where the retailer displays products and collects customer orders, but inventory is stored entirely at the manufacturer’s facility and shipped directly to the consumer, completely bypassing retailer warehouses.
- **In-Transit Merge:** A distribution architecture that synchronizes components manufactured at disparate supplier locations (e.g., a computer CPU from Factory A and a monitor from Factory B) at a regional freight carrier merge hub so the customer receives a single consolidated delivery.
- **Last-Mile Fleet Delivery:** Distribution where regional warehouses utilize dedicated proprietary vehicle fleets (rather than third-party parcel carriers) to deliver orders directly to consumer homes.
- **Click-and-Collect (Customer Pickup):** Omnichannel fulfillment where orders placed online are dispatched to convenient local pickup lockers or retail stores, with the customer executing the physical last-mile collection.

---

## 2. Important Points

### The Six Foundational Distribution Network Designs
The lecture examines the structural mechanics and performance trade-offs of the six classical distribution models:

#### 1. Manufacturer Storage with Direct Shipping (Drop-Shipping)
- *Inventory:* Maximizes inventory pooling; centralized at the manufacturer, eliminating retail safety stock.
- *Transportation:* High outbound parcel shipping costs; split shipments if an order spans multiple manufacturers.
- *Response Time:* Long lead times (**1 to 2 weeks**).
- *Best Application:* High-value, low-demand, highly customized SKUs with high profit margins.

#### 2. Manufacturer Storage with In-Transit Merge
- *Mechanics:* Multiple product sub-components from separate plants are merged in-transit at a regional logistics facility.
- *Advantage:* Lowers customer shipping confusion and packaging waste by consolidating deliveries; lowers final delivery transport cost compared to pure drop-shipping.
- *Prerequisite:* Highly sophisticated IT coordination and carrier telematics.

#### 3. Distributor Storage with Package Carrier Delivery
- *Mechanics:* Wholesalers and distributors warehouse products closer to consumers and dispatch via express parcel carriers (e.g., DHL, FedEx, Blue Dart).
- *Performance:* Shorter response times (**1 to 3 business days**) than drop-shipping; higher inventory holding costs due to regional stocking.

#### 4. Distributor Storage with Last-Mile Delivery
- *Mechanics:* The distributor operates a dedicated delivery fleet to deliver goods directly to the customer’s doorstep within hours (e.g., localized grocery delivery, B2B auto parts).
- *Performance:* Fast response time (**same-day / within hours**); very high transportation costs requiring high order density to be economically viable.

#### 5. Manufacturer / Distributor Storage with Customer Pickup
- *Mechanics:* Inventory is transported from central hubs to designated pickup sites (e.g., 24/7 automated parcel lockers, neighborhood convenience stores, local gas stations).
- *Advantage:* Dramatically slashes last-mile carrier delivery expenses because the customer provides final transport; eliminates failed doorstep delivery attempts.

#### 6. Retail Storage with Customer Pickup
- *Mechanics:* Traditional brick-and-mortar retail stores where customer browses, purchases, and transports goods immediately.
- *Performance:* **Fastest possible response time (instantaneous possession)**; highest facility lease costs and highest aggregate safety inventory costs across the supply chain.

## 3. Differences / Examples

### Comparative Evaluation of the Six Distribution Network Designs

| Network Design Option | Response Time | Inventory Costs | Transportation Costs | Facility Overhead | Best Product Category |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Drop-Shipping** | Long (1–2 weeks) | **Lowest (Centralized)** | **High (Parcel rates)** | Very Low | Slow-moving, high-value, customizable items |
| **2. In-Transit Merge** | Moderate (3–7 days) | Low to Moderate | Moderate | Low | High-value multi-component systems (PCs) |
| **3. Distributor Storage / Carrier**| Fast (1–3 days) | Moderate | Moderate to High | Moderate | Fast-to-medium velocity consumer items |
| **4. Distributor / Last-Mile Fleet**| **Very Fast (Same-day)**| Moderate to High | **Highest (Fleet cost)**| Moderate to High | Perishables, groceries, high-density urban items |
| **5. Storage / Customer Pickup** | Moderate (1–2 days) | Moderate | **Lowest (Shared freight)**| Moderate | E-commerce parcels with high daytime absence |
| **6. Retail Storage / Store Pickup**| **Instantaneous** | **Highest (Decentralized)**| Low (Bulk FTL to store)| **Highest (Retail leases)**| Standard fast-moving retail staples (FMCG) |

## 4. Must Remember

- The six core distribution designs are: **Drop-Shipping, In-Transit Merge, Distributor Storage with Carrier Delivery, Distributor Storage with Last-Mile Fleet, Storage with Customer Pickup, and Retail Storage with Customer Pickup**.
- **Drop-shipping** provides maximum inventory pooling (lowest inventory cost) but incurs high parcel transport costs and longer response times.
- **In-transit merge** consolidates items from multiple manufacturers at a carrier terminal so the customer receives a single unified package.
- **Retail storage with customer pickup** delivers **instantaneous response time**, but requires the **highest inventory and facility investment**.
- **Customer pickup (Click-and-Collect)** eliminates expensive last-mile carrier delivery costs and completely eliminates failed residential delivery attempts.
- Network selection must match product velocity: **slow-moving, high-value goods fit drop-shipping**, while **fast-moving, low-margin goods fit retail/distributor storage**.
