# Week 9 – Lecture 2: Designing Distribution Networks in Supply Chain

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Distribution Network Design:** Selecting the structural combination of facility storage locations and physical transportation routes to deliver products from factories to end users.
- **Drop-Shipping (Direct Shipping):** A fulfillment model where product inventory is held exclusively at the manufacturer's central plant and shipped directly to retail consumers upon order placement.
- **In-Transit Merge:** A distribution technique where separate components of an order originating from distinct manufacturing sites are merged at a transit hub so the customer receives a single combined shipment.

## 2. Important Points
- **Six Primary Distribution Network Architectures (Chopra & Meindl):**
  1. **Manufacturer Storage with Direct Delivery (Drop-Shipping):** Centralizes inventory; low inventory cost; supports infinite product variety; long response times and complex return handling.
  2. **Manufacturer Storage with Direct Delivery and In-Transit Merge:** Combines items from multiple factories (e.g., PC tower from one plant, monitor from another) without holding intermediate warehouse inventory.
  3. **Distributor Storage with Package Carrier Delivery:** Inventory held at regional distributor warehouses; fulfilled via commercial parcel couriers (e.g., FedEx, UPS); faster response time, higher inventory costs.
  4. **Distributor Storage with Last-Mile Delivery:** Distributor operates its own dedicated vehicle fleet delivering to customer doorsteps; very fast response; economically viable only for high customer density (e.g., online grocery).
  5. **Manufacturer / Distributor Storage with Customer Pickup:** Orders shipped to local designated pickup lockers or retail partner sites where customers retrieve goods at their convenience (BOPIS).
  6. **Retail Storage with Customer Pickup:** Traditional retail shopping; highest inventory costs; immediate product availability for consumer; zero outbound freight cost for firm.

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Network Design Option | Inventory Cost | Transportation Cost | Response Time | Product Variety |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Drop-Shipping** | Very Low | Very High | Long ($3\text{–}7\text{ days}$) | Very High |
| **Distributor Storage with Carrier** | Medium | Medium | Fast ($1\text{–}2\text{ days}$) | Medium |
| **Retail Store Pickup** | Very High | Low | Immediate ($0\text{ days}$) | Low |

## 4. Must Remember
- High-value, low-demand, slow-moving items are best distributed via **Drop-Shipping** to avoid holding expensive inventory across multiple regions.
- Fast-moving, bulky commodity goods require **Distributor or Retail Storage** close to consumption markets to contain high transportation freight costs.
