# Week 8 – Lecture 1: Strategic Sourcing

## 1. Key Concepts

- **Strategic Sourcing:** The institutional procurement methodology that systematically analyzes an organization’s spending profiles, supplier markets, and component risks to select, develop, and manage suppliers in alignment with enterprise competitive strategy.
- **The Kraljic Portfolio Matrix:** A landmark procurement framework formalized by Peter Kraljic that categorizes purchased items and supplier relationships along two fundamental dimensions: **Profit Impact** and **Supply Risk**.
- **Profit Impact (Financial Exposure):** The degree to which an item directly affects the enterprise's bottom-line profitability, total cost of goods sold ($COGS$), volume purchased, or end-product differentiation.
- **Supply Risk (Market Vulnerability):** The complexity of the supply market, characterized by supplier scarcity, geographic concentration, technological barriers to entry, logistics bottlenecks, or availability of substitute materials.
- **Early Supplier Involvement (ESI):** Integrating key strategic suppliers into the earliest conceptual and design phases of new product development to capture supplier engineering insights, reduce manufacturing costs, and accelerate time-to-market.

## 2. Important Points

### The Kraljic Portfolio Matrix Architecture
The lecture details the four quadrants of the Kraljic Matrix, specifying tailored sourcing tactics for each:

```
                              PROFIT IMPACT
                       Low                         High
           ┌───────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────┐
      High │ 2. BOTTLENECK ITEMS       │ 1. STRATEGIC ITEMS        │
S          │ • Low profit, high risk   │ • High profit, high risk  │
U          │ • Scarce, unique parts    │ • Core engines, microchips│
P          │ ──► Strategy: Secure      │ ──► Strategy: Long-Term   │
P          │     Supply & Buffer Stock │     Strategic Partnership │
L          ├───────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤
Y     Low  │ 4. ROUTINE / NON-CRITICAL │ 3. LEVERAGE ITEMS         │
R          │ • Low profit, low risk    │ • High profit, low risk   │
I          │ • Office supplies, bolts  │ • Bulk steel, packaging   │
S          │ ──► Strategy: Automate &  │ ──► Strategy: Competitive │
K          │     Minimize Admin Cost   │     Bidding & Auctions    │
           └───────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┘
```

#### 1. Strategic Items (High Profit Impact, High Supply Risk)
- *Product Profile:* Highly complex, customized, proprietary sub-assemblies critical to product differentiation (e.g., automobile engine blocks, aircraft turbine blades, advanced semiconductor chipsets).
- *Market Dynamics:* Very few qualified global suppliers; high technological and switching costs.
- *Recommended Strategy:* Establish **long-term strategic collaborative partnerships**, practice Early Supplier Involvement ($ESI$), execute open-book costing, and develop joint R&D roadmaps.

#### 2. Bottleneck Items (Low Profit Impact, High Supply Risk)
- *Product Profile:* Inexpensive components that nevertheless possess proprietary designs or single-source dependency (e.g., specialized chemical additives, unique patented micro-switches, custom fasteners).
- *Market Dynamics:* Monopolistic or geographically remote supplier base; stockouts halt the entire assembly line despite low component value.
- *Recommended Strategy:* **Secure supply continuity at all costs**—maintain high safety stock buffers, sign long-term supply guarantee contracts, and actively re-engineer products to substitute standard components.

#### 3. Leverage Items (High Profit Impact, Low Supply Risk)
- *Product Profile:* High-volume, high-spend commodities with standardized industry specifications (e.g., structural steel sheets, bulk commodity plastics, corrugated shipping cartons, standard fuel).
- *Market Dynamics:* Abundant competing suppliers; low switching costs.
- *Recommended Strategy:* **Exploit commercial purchasing power**—conduct competitive tenders, reverse e-auctions, split orders across vendors, and negotiate volume-based rebate agreements.

#### 4. Non-Critical / Routine Items (Low Profit Impact, Low Supply Risk)
- *Product Profile:* Everyday, low-value operating consumables and MRO items (e.g., cleaning supplies, standard nuts and bolts, office stationery).
- *Market Dynamics:* Ubiquitous supply; minimal financial risk.
- *Recommended Strategy:* **Standardize product specifications and automate procurement**—deploy digital e-catalogs, procurement cards (P-cards), and blanket purchase orders to minimize administrative processing expense.

> **Mnemonic for the Kraljic Matrix Quadrants (Assistant-Created):**  
> Remember **S-B-L-R**: **S**trategic (Partner), **B**ottleneck (Buffer), **L**everage (Compete), **R**outine (Automate).

## 3. Differences / Examples

### Synthesis: The Kraljic Sourcing Strategies

| Category | Sourcing Tactic | Supplier Relationship | Primary Operational Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Strategic Items** | Long-term alliance, joint R&D | Deep collaborative partnership | Mutual competitive differentiation; secure capacity. |
| **Bottleneck Items** | Buffer inventory, search substitutes | Guaranteed capacity contracts | **Eliminate disruption risk and line shutdowns**. |
| **Leverage Items** | Reverse auctions, volume tenders | Transactional / Competitive bidding | **Aggressive unit price minimization**. |
| **Routine Items** | E-procurement catalogs, P-cards | Decentralized purchasing | **Minimize internal administrative processing cost**. |

## 4. Must Remember

- The **Kraljic Portfolio Matrix** classifies purchased items across two axes: **Profit Impact** and **Supply Risk**.
- The four quadrants are **Strategic Items (High/High), Bottleneck Items (Low Profit/High Risk), Leverage Items (High Profit/Low Risk), and Routine/Non-Critical Items (Low/Low)**.
- **Strategic items** require **long-term collaborative partnerships and Early Supplier Involvement (ESI)**.
- **Bottleneck items** threaten assembly line continuity; strategy focuses on **securing supply, holding safety buffers, and searching for substitutes**.
- **Leverage items** allow the firm to **exploit purchasing power via competitive bidding and reverse auctions**.
- **Routine items** are managed by **automating e-procurement and using P-cards** to slash administrative processing costs.
- Sourcing strategy must never treat all suppliers identically; category management demands quadrant-specific procurement tactics.
