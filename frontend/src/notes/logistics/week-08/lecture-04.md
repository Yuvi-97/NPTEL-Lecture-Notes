# Week 8 – Lecture 4: Supply Chain Performance: Balanced Scorecard Approach

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Balanced Scorecard (BSC):** A strategic management and performance evaluation framework developed by Robert S. Kaplan and David P. Norton (1992) that translates an organization’s mission and strategy into comprehensive physical and financial metrics.
- **Flaws of Traditional Accounting Metrics:** Traditional financial metrics (e.g., ROI, quarterly net profit) are backward-looking **lagging indicators** that measure past history but fail to capture the operational drivers of future competitive advantage.
- **Strategic Balance:** Harmonizes four critical operational dimensions: financial vs. non-financial, short-term vs. long-term, internal operational processes vs. external stakeholder satisfaction, and lagging vs. leading indicators.

## 2. Important Points
- **The Four Perspectives of the Balanced Scorecard:**
  1. **Financial Perspective ("How do we look to shareholders?"):**
     - Return on Capital Employed (ROCE), Operating Margin, Cash-to-Cash Cycle Time, Total Supply Chain Management Cost as $\%$ of revenue.
  2. **Customer Perspective ("How do customers see us?"):**
     - On-Time In-Full (OTIF) delivery rate, customer order fulfillment lead time, order defect rate, customer retention percentage.
  3. **Internal Business Process Perspective ("What must we excel at?"):**
     - Manufacturing cycle efficiency, warehouse order picking accuracy, supplier quality yield, inventory turns, dock-to-stock time.
  4. **Learning and Growth Perspective ("How can we continue to improve and create value?"):**
     - Workforce multi-skilling, safety training hours, digital technology adoption (RFID/WMS), employee turnover, supplier collaboration capability.

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Perspective | Question Addressed | Typical Supply Chain KPI |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Financial** | How do we create shareholder value? | Cash-to-Cash cycle time, total logistics spend |
| **Customer** | How do we satisfy our market? | Perfect Order Index (POI), customer lead time |
| **Internal Processes** | What operational workflows must excel? | Order pick cycle time, warehouse capacity utilization |
| **Learning & Growth**| How do we sustain future innovation? | Supply chain digital skills training, employee retention |

## 4. Must Remember
- Reference developers: **Robert S. Kaplan and David P. Norton (1992)**, *The Balanced Scorecard: Measures That Drive Performance*, Harvard Business Review.
- Non-financial metrics (customer satisfaction, process efficiency) serve as **leading indicators** that predict future financial profitability.
