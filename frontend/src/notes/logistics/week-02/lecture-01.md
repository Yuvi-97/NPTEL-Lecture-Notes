# Week 2 – Lecture 1: E-Logistics

## 1. Key Concepts
- **E-Logistics:** The specialized logistics and supply chain systems designed to support online commerce (B2C, B2B, C2C), managing the digital flow of information and physical parcel fulfillment.
- **Last-Mile Delivery:** The final, most expensive leg of logistics moving goods from a local fulfillment center to the end customer's doorstep, accounting for up to $40\text{–}50\%$ of total shipping costs.
- **Customer Expectations in E-Commerce:** Demands for real-time order tracking, narrow delivery time windows, same-day delivery, Cash on Delivery (COD) options, and frictionless product returns.

## 2. Important Points
- **Key Challenges in E-Logistics:**
  - *Order Fragmentation:* Processing millions of individual single-item parcel orders rather than bulk palletized shipments.
  - *Delivery Speed Pressure:* Managing hyper-local hubs and micro-fulfillment centers to enable same-day or instant delivery.
  - *High Return Rates:* Apparel and electronics return rates often reach $20\text{–}30\%$, requiring efficient reverse logistics.
  - *Failed Deliveries:* High costs incurred when customer is unavailable at the delivery address during first attempt.
- **Core Technological Enablers:**
  - Warehouse Management Systems (WMS) with barcode/RFID scanning.
  - Dynamic route optimization and GPS vehicle tracking.
  - Automated sorting centers with automated guided vehicles (AGVs).

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Parameter | Traditional Retail Logistics | E-Logistics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Shipment Size** | Bulk quantities (truckloads, pallets) | Small parcels (single items) |
| **Destination** | Fixed physical retail stores | Highly dispersed residential doorsteps |
| **Demand Pattern** | Predictable replenishment orders | Highly erratic, promotion-driven peaks |
| **Reverse Flow** | Low ($< 3\%$, damaged stock returns) | Extremely high ($20\text{–}30\%$ customer returns) |
