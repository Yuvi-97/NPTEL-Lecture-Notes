# Week 2 – Lecture 2: International Logistics

## 1. Key Concepts

- **International Logistics:** The strategic design, management, and operational control of cross-border material, service, and information flows across international boundaries, navigating divergent legal jurisdictions, customs tariffs, currency regimes, and multimodal transport corridors.
- **Incoterms (International Commercial Terms):** Globally standardized contractual rules published by the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC) defining the precise division of costs, risks, operational obligations, and handover points between buyers (importers) and sellers (exporters).
- **Consolidator:** A specialized intermediary that leases or purchases large blocks of vessel or aircraft cargo space and subdivides it into smaller Less-than-Container Load ($LCL$) shipments for multiple small-to-medium shippers.
- **Freight Forwarder:** A third-party logistics architect who orchestrates end-to-end multi-modal routing, documentation, cargo space booking, and customs compliance on behalf of shippers without necessarily owning the underlying vessels.
- **Cross-Border Transaction Friction:** Delays and costs incurred during international trade due to intermediary bank fees, foreign exchange spreads, 10-to-12-hour time-zone settlement lags, truncated data formats, and complex regulatory sanction screenings.

## 2. Important Points

### Complete Stakeholder Map in International Logistics
International shipping involves a complex multi-party ecosystem:
1. **Exporter (Shipper):** Origin seller responsible for manufacturing, export packaging, and commercial invoicing.
2. **Importer (Consignee):** Destination buyer receiving goods, paying tariffs, and managing domestic distribution.
3. **Freight Forwarder:** Logistics intermediary organizing multimodal transit legs, export declarations, and freight scheduling.
4. **Customs Agent / Broker:** Licensed specialist preparing statutory customs documentation, duty calculations, and physical cargo clearance at border points.
5. **Shipping Company (Ocean Carrier):** The legal owner/operator of commercial vessels, container ships, or cargo fleets.
6. **Shipping Agent:** The legal port representative of the shipping line handling berthing, port authority clearances, customs manifests, and vessel turnaround.
7. **Consolidator:** Aggregates small, fragmented consignments from multiple shippers into full containers ($FCL$) to achieve freight economies of scale.
8. **Physical Carriers:** Inland rail rakes, intermodal trucking fleets, and air cargo operators executing specific transport legs.
9. **Marine Insurance Underwriters:** Assesses voyage transit risks and provides financial coverage against cargo loss, damage, theft, or general average.
10. **Commercial and Merchant Banks:** Issues Letters of Credit ($LC$), verifies shipping documents against trade terms, and reconciles foreign exchange settlements.

### Key Financial Metrics in International Supply Chains
- **Inventory Turnover Ratio ($ITR$):**
  Measures the efficiency with which international inventory is circulated and sold over a specified operating cycle:

  $$\text{Inventory Turnover} = \frac{\text{Total Sales (Cost of Goods Sold)}}{\text{Average Inventory}}$$

- **Average Inventory Calculation:**

  $$\text{Average Inventory} = \frac{\text{Beginning Inventory} + \text{Ending Inventory}}{2}$$

### Mechanics of Currency Fluctuations in Global Trade
Fluctuations in foreign exchange rates directly alter import-export profitability margins:
- *Baseline Scenario:* Assume an exchange rate of $1\text{ Euro} = 100\text{ INR}$ (€1 = Rs. 100).
- **Scenario A: Domestic Currency Depreciates ($1\text{ Euro} = 105\text{ INR}$):**
  - *Importer Impact:* The domestic importer must now pay **Rs. 105** for the same €1 item (Rs. 5 extra cost), reducing import profitability and suppressing domestic import demand.
  - *Exporter Impact:* The domestic exporter receives **Rs. 105** upon converting €1 foreign revenue (Rs. 5 bonus), increasing export profit margins and enhancing global price competitiveness.
- **Scenario B: Domestic Currency Appreciates ($1\text{ Euro} = 95\text{ INR}$):**
  - *Importer Impact:* The domestic importer pays only **Rs. 95** per €1 item, making foreign sourcing cheaper.
  - *Exporter Impact:* The domestic exporter receives only **Rs. 95** per €1 item, eroding export margins.

### Primary Operational Barriers in Cross-Border Trade
1. **Financial Intermediary Fees:** Tiered merchant processing, correspondent banking fees, and currency conversion markups.
2. **Reconciliation Time Lags:** Global banking networks introduce 10-to-12-hour settlement windows between eastern and western time zones.
3. **Fragmented Data Formats:** Inconsistent international address structures, encoding variances, and manual data transcription errors between disparate legacy IT systems.
4. **Sanctions and Compliance Screening:** Automated fraud algorithms frequently flag false positives due to common names or phonetic matches, temporarily freezing cargo shipments.
5. **Cross-Border Dispute Jurisdiction:** Disagreements regarding cargo damage or delivery shortfalls require pre-agreed international arbitration venues established via Incoterms.

## 3. Differences / Examples

### Impact of Exchange Rate Movements on Global Trade

| Economic Event | Impact on Domestic Importers | Impact on Domestic Exporters |
|---|---|---|
| **Domestic Currency Depreciates** (e.g., ₹ drops from ₹100/€ to ₹105/€) | **Negative:** Imported goods become more expensive (+₹5/€); margins shrink. | **Positive:** Foreign revenues yield higher domestic currency (+₹5/€); boosts competitiveness. |
| **Domestic Currency Appreciates** (e.g., ₹ rises from ₹100/€ to ₹95/€) | **Positive:** Sourcing foreign materials becomes cheaper (-₹5/€); lowers landed cost. | **Negative:** Foreign revenue yields less domestic currency (-₹5/€); compresses export margins. |

## 4. Must Remember

- The primary metric evaluating inventory velocity is **Inventory Turnover**:
  $$\text{Inventory Turnover} = \frac{\text{Total Sales (COGS)}}{\text{Average Inventory}}$$
  where $\text{Average Inventory} = \frac{\text{Beginning Inventory} + \text{Ending Inventory}}{2}$.
- **Currency depreciation benefits domestic exporters** (higher domestic revenue per foreign unit) but **harms domestic importers** (higher landed procurement costs).
- **Currency appreciation benefits domestic importers** (cheaper procurement) but **harms domestic exporters** (lower domestic revenue per foreign unit).
- **Incoterms** establish the contractual boundary for the transfer of transport costs, physical risks, and legal responsibilities between the exporter and importer.
- A **Freight Forwarder** acts as a multimodal travel agent for freight, while a **Consolidator** aggregates small Less-than-Container Load ($LCL$) shipments into Full Container Loads ($FCL$).
- Cross-border logistics involves ten critical stakeholders: **Exporter, Importer, Freight Forwarder, Customs Agent, Shipping Line, Shipping Agent, Consolidator, Carriers, Marine Insurers, and Commercial Banks**.
- Operational barriers in international trade include **10–12 hour banking time-zone lags, currency conversion fees, fragmented legacy data formats, and international sanction screening delays**.
