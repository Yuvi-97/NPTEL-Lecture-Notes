# Week 2 – Lecture 2: International Logistics

## 1. Key Concepts
- **International Logistics:** The design and management of global supply chain networks handling the cross-border flow of physical goods, financial capital, and documentation across national boundaries.
- **Cross-Border Complexity:** Heightened logistics risks resulting from tariff duties, customs clearance barriers, differing legal jurisdictions, currency exchange volatility, and lengthy transit lead times.
- **Key Intermediaries:** Third-party specialists including **Freight Forwarders** (arranging multimodal transport and booking cargo space) and **Customs Brokers** (handling import/export clearance and duty documentation).

## 2. Important Points
- **Cross-Border Financial Payment Mechanisms:**
  1. *Cash in Advance:* Importer pays before goods are shipped (maximum risk for buyer, zero risk for exporter).
  2. *Letter of Credit (LC):* A formal financial commitment issued by the importer's bank guaranteeing payment to the exporter upon presentation of compliant shipping documents (most secure, balanced mechanism).
  3. *Documentary Collection (Drafts):* Commercial banks act as intermediaries to exchange shipping documents (e.g., Bill of Lading) against payment (D/P) or acceptance of bill of exchange (D/A).
  4. *Open Account:* Exporter ships goods before receiving payment (maximum risk for exporter, preferred by established buyers).
- **Major Stakeholders:** Exporter/Shipper, Importer/Consignee, Commercial Banks, Ocean/Air Carriers, Port Terminal Operators, and Customs Authorities.

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Payment Method | Risk to Exporter | Risk to Importer | Common Usage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cash in Advance** | Lowest | Highest | New buyers, politically unstable markets |
| **Letter of Credit (LC)** | Very Low (Bank-backed) | Low (Pay on document proof) | High-value international commodity consignments |
| **Open Account** | Highest | Lowest | Long-term trusted multinational trading partners |

## 4. Must Remember
- The **Bill of Lading (B/L)** serves three legal functions: document of title to the goods, contract of carriage between shipper and carrier, and receipt of cargo.
- Letter of Credit (LC) balances cross-border payment risk by making financial settlement contingent on verified documentation rather than buyer whim.
