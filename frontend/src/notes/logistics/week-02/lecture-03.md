# Week 2 – Lecture 3: International Logistics (Market Penetration Strategies)

## 1. Key Concepts
- **International Market Entry Strategies:** Strategic pathways through which enterprises expand their logistics and business operations into foreign territories, trading off capital commitment, operational control, and financial risk.
- **Incoterms (International Commercial Terms):** Standardized three-letter trade definitions published by the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC) defining the exact point where costs, risks, and transport obligations transfer from seller to buyer.
- **Countertrade:** International reciprocal trade arrangements where payment is made partially or entirely in alternative goods rather than hard convertible currency.

## 2. Important Points
- **Hierarchy of Market Entry Modes (Low to High Commitment):**
  1. **Direct / Indirect Exporting:** Manufacturing domestically and selling abroad; minimal capital expenditure, low risk, but high tariff and freight costs.
  2. **Licensing & Franchising:** Granting foreign entities legal rights to manufacture products or operate business models for royalty fees; low capital, risk of creating competitors.
  3. **Joint Ventures (JVs):** Sharing equity and operational control with a local foreign partner; combines local regulatory knowledge with foreign technology.
  4. **Wholly Owned Subsidiaries / FDI:** Establishing greenfield facilities or acquiring local firms; maximum operational control and profit retention, but highest financial and political risk.
- **Common Incoterms Categories:**
  - *EXW (Ex Works):* Buyer assumes all shipping costs and risks from the seller's factory gate.
  - *FOB (Free On Board):* Seller covers transport until goods pass ship's rail at loading port.
  - *CIF (Cost, Insurance, and Freight):* Seller pays ocean freight and marine insurance to destination port.
  - *DDP (Delivered Duty Paid):* Maximum obligation on seller; covers all freight, insurance, and import duties to buyer's door.

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Entry Strategy | Capital Investment | Operational Control | Risk Exposure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Exporting** | Low | Low | Low (currency & transport risk only) |
| **Joint Venture** | Medium | Shared 50/50 | Medium (partner conflict risk) |
| **Wholly Owned Subsidiary** | High | High (100% control) | High (expropriation & market risk) |

## 4. Must Remember
- **Incoterms** govern risk and freight cost allocation; they do NOT determine property ownership or contract law breach remedies.
- As entry strategy moves from Exporting $\to$ Joint Venture $\to$ FDI, logistics operational control increases, but capital exposure grows dramatically.
