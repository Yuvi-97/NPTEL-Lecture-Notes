# Week 4 – Lecture 3: Logistics vs. Supply Chain Management

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Logistics Management:** The operational discipline that plans, implements, and controls the efficient forward and reverse flow and storage of goods, services, and related information between point of origin and point of consumption.
- **Supply Chain Management (SCM):** The overarching strategic coordination and integration of all business processes—from raw material sourcing and manufacturing conversion through final customer fulfillment—across an extended network of independent enterprise partners.
- **Structural Relationship:** **Logistics is an integral functional subset of Supply Chain Management.** All logistics is part of SCM, but SCM encompasses significantly more than logistics.

## 2. Important Points
- **Official Definition (CSCMP - Council of Supply Chain Management Professionals):**
  - *SCM* encompasses the planning and management of all sourcing, procurement, conversion, and logistics activities, alongside active collaboration and coordination with channel partners (suppliers, intermediaries, third-party service providers, and customers).
- **Core Logistical Components within SCM:**
  - Transportation management and carrier selection.
  - Warehousing and storage management.
  - Materials handling and industrial packaging.
  - Inventory management and deployment.
  - Order processing and fulfillment tracking.
- **Non-Logistical Elements Unique to SCM:**
  - Strategic supplier relationship management (SRM).
  - New product co-development and engineering design collaboration.
  - Long-term strategic capacity planning and facility location.
  - Demand planning and collaborative sales and operations planning (S&OP).
  - Pricing, contract negotiation, and customer relationship management (CRM).

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Parameter | Logistics Management | Supply Chain Management (SCM) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Organizational Scope** | Primarily intra-organizational (single firm operations) | Inter-organizational (network of independent partnering firms) |
| **Operational Focus** | Movement and storage of products & materials | Total business process integration across supplier-to-customer chain |
| **Primary Objective** | Cost efficiency & meeting immediate delivery service levels | Maximizing competitive advantage & total chain surplus/value |
| **Evolutionary Era** | Older historical discipline (rooted in military transport) | Modern management concept (emerged prominently in 1980s–1990s) |

## 4. Must Remember
- **Hierarchical Truth:** Logistics is an **operational component** inside the strategic framework of Supply Chain Management.
- SCM focuses on building long-term collaborative trust and win-win relationships across multiple firms; logistics focuses on physical execution.
