# Week 4 – Lecture 2: Trends in Logistics (Contd.)

## 1. Key Concepts

- **Supply Chain Resilience:** The adaptive operational capacity of a logistics network to anticipate, prepare for, respond to, and rapidly recover from unexpected disruptions (natural disasters, pandemics, geopolitical conflicts, or supplier bankruptcies) while maintaining continuity of customer fulfillment.
- **Predictive Analytics & Anticipatory Logistics:** Leveraging historical order data, browsing telemetry, weather forecasts, and machine learning models to forecast precisely which products will be ordered, in what quantities, and at what locations *before* the consumer actually clicks the purchase button.
- **Anticipatory Shipping:** A proprietary logistics strategy patented by leading e-commerce players (e.g., Amazon) where goods are physically packaged and dispatched toward localized delivery hubs before the customer places the order, enabling sub-2-hour fulfillment.
- **Fleet Electrification:** The large-scale operational transition of delivery vehicle fleets from internal combustion diesel/petrol engines to battery-electric vehicles ($BEVs$) to achieve corporate carbon-neutrality targets.
- **The Up-skilling Dilemma:** The critical labor transition challenge wherein warehouse automation does not simply eliminate jobs, but shifts labor demand from manual material handlers to specialized mechatronics technicians, robotics programmers, and WMS software analysts.

## 2. Important Points

### Macro Survey Data on Supply Chain Disruptions
The lecture highlights findings from comprehensive global supply chain surveys:
- **Disruption Prevalence:** Over **80% of organizations** experienced major operational supply chain disruptions over recent reporting cycles.
- **Severe Partner Failures:** **27% of survey respondents reported that 11 or more critical trading partners failed** during systemic disruptions (pandemics, trade wars, severe port blockages).
- **Primary Vulnerability:** Heavy reliance on single-source suppliers and non-transparent multi-tier supply networks lacking real-time visibility.

### Strategies for Building Supply Chain Resilience
1. **Multi-Sourcing & Nearshoring:**
   - Shifting from single-source global procurement to a "China + 1" or regional nearshoring model.
   - Maintaining qualified secondary and tertiary suppliers who can instantly absorb order volume if a primary vendor fails.
2. **Dynamic Risk Monitoring & Visibility:**
   - Real-time GPS and control tower platforms continuously monitoring weather disruptions, port strikes, and geopolitical flashpoints to reroute shipments proactively.
3. **Strategic Buffer Stocks:**
   - Re-evaluating pure Just-in-Time (JIT) zero-inventory models; maintaining selective safety stocks for mission-critical, long-lead components ("Just-in-Case" hybrid buffers).

### Fleet Electrification and Sustainability Targets
- **Amazon Fleet Pledge:** Targeted converting **100% of its global delivery fleet to electric vehicles by 2030**, deploying over 100,000 custom electric delivery vans.
- **Operational Benefits of EVs:**
  - Zero tailpipe greenhouse gas emissions ($CO_2$, $NO_x$, particulate matter) in dense urban corridors.
  - Substantially lower maintenance and per-kilometer fuel/energy running costs compared to diesel vans.
  - Quiet night-time deliveries permitted in noise-restricted residential neighborhoods.

### Hyper-Local Fulfillment Networks and Dark Stores
- **The 10-to-20-Minute Fulfillment Model (e.g., Swiggy Instamart, Zepto, Blinkit):**
  - Traditional centralized distribution centers located 30–50 km outside city limits cannot physically fulfill orders within 30 minutes.
  - Quick-commerce models deploy hundreds of small, localized micro-fulfillment hubs ("dark stores") across urban residential neighborhoods, each serving a tight 2-to-3 km radius.
  - Dark stores carry high-velocity, standardized SKUs (milk, bread, snacks, OTC medicines) optimized for instantaneous picking and dispatch on two-wheelers.

## 3. Differences / Examples

### Traditional Logistics vs. Modern Predictive Logistics

| Dimension | Traditional Logistics | Modern Predictive Logistics |
|---|---|---|
| **Demand Trigger** | Reactive; wait for confirmed customer purchase order. | **Proactive; algorithmic forecasting and anticipatory shipping**. |
| **Inventory Positioning**| Stored in distant, centralized regional warehouses. | Positioned in urban micro-fulfillment hubs / dark stores near buyer. |
| **Delivery Timeframe** | 2 to 5 business days. | **Same-day, 1-hour, or 10–20 minute hyper-local delivery**. |
| **Fleet Power Source** | Diesel internal combustion trucks. | Electric delivery vans ($EVs$) and e-scooters. |
| **Disruption Response**| Manual expediting, firefighting, delayed awareness. | Automated algorithmic rerouting and digital twin simulation. |

## 4. Must Remember

- Over **80% of global enterprises** experience major supply chain disruptions; **27% reported that 11 or more trading partners failed** during crises, emphasizing the need for resilience.
- **Supply chain resilience** requires multi-sourcing, dynamic control tower visibility, and a transition from pure JIT to selective "Just-in-Case" buffers.
- **Predictive analytics and anticipatory shipping** move inventory to local delivery hubs *before* the customer places an order, enabling sub-same-day delivery.
- **Fleet electrification** (e.g., Amazon’s target of a 100% electric delivery fleet by 2030) reduces running costs, eliminates urban emissions, and enables quiet night deliveries.
- Quick commerce achieves **10–20 minute delivery** by replacing distant mega-warehouses with dense networks of **urban micro-fulfillment centers (dark stores)**.
- Automation creates an **up-skilling requirement**: warehouse labor transitions from manual handlers to robotics operators, mechatronics technicians, and data analysts.
