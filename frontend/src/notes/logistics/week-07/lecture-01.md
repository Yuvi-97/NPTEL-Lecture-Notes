# Week 7 – Lecture 1: Supply Chain Impellers and Value Chain

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Supply Chain Impellers:** Powerful external and internal catalytic forces that compel organizations to continually revise, innovate, and reconfigure their supply chain architecture to survive.
- **Michael Porter’s Value Chain (1985):** A foundational framework dividing an enterprise into discrete primary and support activities that add customer-perceived value to products and create profit margin.
- **Supply Chain Margin:** The economic value generated across the supply chain minus the collective operational expenses incurred in executing the value chain activities.

## 2. Important Points
- **Primary Supply Chain Impellers:**
  - *Rapid Technological Disruptions:* Cloud platforms, IoT tracking, predictive analytics, and automated robotics.
  - *Globalization & Geopolitics:* Trade tariff wars, shifting trade corridors, and regional protectionism.
  - *Evolving Consumer Lifestyles:* Demand for instant delivery, hyper-customization, and hassle-free returns.
  - *Sustainability & ESG Mandates:* Government environmental regulations, carbon taxes, and Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR).
  - *Competitive Pressures:* Rival firms compressing order fulfillment times and undercutting delivery fees.
- **Structure of Porter’s Value Chain:**
  - **Primary Activities (Direct Flow of Value):**
    1. *Inbound Logistics:* Receiving, storing, and inventory control of raw materials.
    2. *Operations:* Machining, assembly, packaging, and testing.
    3. *Outbound Logistics:* Warehousing, order processing, and delivery of finished goods.
    4. *Marketing & Sales:* Advertising, promotion, pricing, and channel selection.
    5. *Service:* Installation, customer support, repair, and warranty handling.
  - **Support Activities (Foundation of Value):**
    - Firm Infrastructure, Human Resource Management, Technology Development, and Procurement.

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Activity Category | Primary Activities | Support Activities |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Operational Role** | Directly transforms, moves, or sells the physical product | Provides essential organizational infrastructure and capabilities |
| **Logistics Presence** | Includes Inbound and Outbound Logistics | Includes Strategic Procurement and IT Infrastructure |

## 4. Must Remember
- Reference text: **Michael E. Porter (1985)**, *Competitive Advantage: Creating and Sustaining Superior Performance*.
- Logistics appears twice as a primary activity in Porter's Value Chain: **Inbound Logistics** (upstream) and **Outbound Logistics** (downstream).
