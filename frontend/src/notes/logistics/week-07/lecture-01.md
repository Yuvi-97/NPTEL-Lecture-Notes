# Week 7 – Lecture 1: Supply Chain Impellers and Value Chain

## 1. Key Concepts

- **Supply Chain Impellers:** The dynamic macro-environmental forces and commercial pressures that compel modern enterprises to abandon fragmented departmental operations and transition toward integrated, responsive supply chain management.
- **Michael Porter’s Value Chain Framework:** A foundational strategic model that conceptualizes an organization as a sequential chain of value-creating activities, partitioned into **Five Primary Activities** (directly involved in physical production and delivery) and **Four Support Activities** (providing the required infrastructure, human talent, technology, and procurement inputs).
- **Value Chain Margin:** The economic value created by the integrated value chain minus the aggregate cost of executing all primary and supporting activities:
  $$\text{Margin} = \text{Total Value Created} - \text{Total Cost of Activities}$$
- **Disintermediation:** The elimination of intermediate distribution tiers (wholesalers, brokers) driven by direct digital communication between producers and consumers.

---

## 2. Important Points

### The Five Major Supply Chain Impellers
1. **Proliferation of Information and Digital Connectivity:** Rapid diffusion of cloud ERP, IoT sensors, high-speed telematics, and real-time POS data sharing connecting disparate global nodes.
2. **Economic Globalization and Trade Liberalization:** Removal of international trade barriers, expansion of multimodal container shipping, and emergence of specialized global manufacturing hubs (e.g., East and South Asia).
3. **Escalating Customer Expectations:** Consumer demands for instantaneous order fulfillment, hyper-personalization, transparent tracking, zero defects, and frictionless return options.
4. **Compression of Product Lifecycles:** Technological obsolescence forces rapid product transitions (especially in electronics and fashion), making inventory holding extremely risky and demanding fast time-to-market.
5. **Environmental Regulations and Sustainability Mandates:** Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) statutes, strict emission quotas, and corporate circular economy commitments.

#### A. Five Primary Activities
1. **Inbound Logistics:** Receiving dock operations, unloading, incoming quality audit, internal inventory slotting, and raw material storage.
2. **Operations (Manufacturing):** Machining, fabrication, chemical processing, electronic assembly, testing, and finished packaging.
3. **Outbound Logistics:** Finished goods warehousing, order picking, staging, loading, linehaul transportation, and distribution delivery.
4. **Marketing and Sales:** Pricing strategy, product promotion, sales channel management, advertising, and commercial proposals.
5. **Service:** Post-sale customer training, installation, scheduled maintenance, warranty replacement, and customer service desks.

#### B. Four Support Activities
1. **Procurement:** Function of purchasing inputs (raw materials, production tooling, office equipment, software licenses) across the entire value chain.
2. **Technology Development:** Equipment engineering, product R&D, laboratory testing, software coding, and digital communications infrastructure.
3. **Human Resource Management:** Recruiting skilled workers, training forklift drivers, safety certifications, wage administration, and labor relations.
4. **Firm Infrastructure:** General administrative management, legal compliance, quality systems, financial auditing, and corporate executive leadership.

## 3. Differences / Examples

### Primary Activities vs. Support Activities in the Value Chain

| Dimension | Primary Activities | Support Activities |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct Role** | Directly involved in the physical creation, sale, transfer, and service of the product. | Provide necessary inputs, infrastructure, and technology to sustain primary activities. |
| **Components** | Inbound Logistics, Operations, Outbound Logistics, Marketing & Sales, Service. | Firm Infrastructure, HR Management, Technology Development, Procurement. |
| **Cost Allocation**| Direct operating expenses ($COGS$, direct freight, direct assembly labor). | Indirect overhead expenses (administrative salaries, R&D budgets, IT leases). |
| **Optimization Focus**| Cycle time reduction, throughput velocity, zero defects, customer satisfaction. | Process standardization, employee up-skilling, strategic vendor development. |

---

## 4. Must Remember

- The five primary impellers of modern SCM are **Digital Information, Globalization, Heightened Customer Expectations, Shortened Product Lifecycles, and Sustainability Regulations**.
- Michael Porter’s Value Chain divides an enterprise into **Five Primary Activities** and **Four Support Activities**.
- The 5 Primary Activities are: **Inbound Logistics, Operations, Outbound Logistics, Marketing & Sales, and Service**.
- The 4 Support Activities are: **Firm Infrastructure, Human Resource Management, Technology Development, and Procurement**.
- **Value Chain Margin** equals the total perceived customer value minus the aggregate cost of executing all primary and supporting activities.
- In modern SCM, **Inbound Logistics, Operations, and Outbound Logistics represent the operational backbone** of Porter’s primary value-creation chain.
