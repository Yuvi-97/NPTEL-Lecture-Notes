# Week 7 – Lecture 4: Achieving Supply Chain Strategic Fit

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Strategic Fit (Chopra & Meindl):** The essential alignment between the customer priorities that the firm’s **Competitive Strategy** seeks to satisfy and the supply chain capabilities that the **Supply Chain Strategy** aims to build.
- **The Implied Demand Uncertainty Spectrum:** The continuum of demand uncertainty that a supply chain is designed to handle, ranging from highly predictable functional products to highly volatile innovative products.
- **The Supply Chain Responsiveness Spectrum:** The continuum of operational capabilities ranging from lowest cost (purely efficient) to maximum agility, flexibility, and speed (purely responsive).

## 2. Important Points
- **The Three Steps to Achieving Strategic Fit:**
  - **Step 1: Understanding Customer and Supply Chain Uncertainty:**
    - Assess customer needs regarding quantity per lot, acceptable response time, product variety, service level required, price sensitivity, and innovation rate.
    - Consolidate these attributes into a single metric: **Implied Demand Uncertainty**.
  - **Step 2: Understanding Supply Chain Capabilities:**
    - Evaluate how the supply chain manages cost vs. responsiveness trade-offs.
    - Locate the supply chain on the **Responsiveness Spectrum** (from purely efficient, such as an oil refinery, to highly responsive, such as an emergency medical courier or custom computer maker).
  - **Step 3: Aligning Uncertainty with Responsiveness (Achieving Fit):**
    - The operational capability must directly match the market demand environment.

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Product Category | Functional Products (e.g., Table Salt) | Innovative Products (e.g., High-End Fashion) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Demand Predictability** | Highly predictable (stable demand) | Highly unpredictable (volatile trends) |
| **Product Life Cycle** | Long ($> 5\text{–}10\text{ years}$) | Short ($3\text{ months to }1\text{ year}$) |
| **Profit Margins** | Low | High |
| **Stockout Costs** | Low customer penalty | Severe lost sales and customer churn |
| **Target Implied Uncertainty** | **Low Implied Uncertainty** | **High Implied Uncertainty** |

## 4. Must Remember
- Reference authors: **Sunil Chopra & Peter Meindl**, *Supply Chain Management*.
- A supply chain strategy that fails to achieve strategic fit risks disaster: applying an efficient, low-cost supply chain to a highly volatile innovative product guarantees stockouts and lost revenue.
