# Week 10 – Lecture 4: Transportation Management

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Transportation Management:** The strategic planning, routing, mode selection, and carrier contracting that moves physical goods between supply chain nodes.
- **Economic Principles of Transportation:**
  1. *Economy of Scale:* Transportation cost per unit weight decreases as shipment size increases (spreading fixed equipment and loading costs over heavier payloads).
  2. *Economy of Distance (Tapering Principle):* Transportation cost per unit distance decreases as trip distance increases (spreading fixed dock and terminal handling expenses over longer hauls).
- **Intermodal Transportation:** Moving freight seamlessly inside standardized ISO shipping containers across multiple successive modes (e.g., ship $\to$ rail $\to$ truck) without direct handling of freight during modal transfers.

## 2. Important Points
- **The Five Basic Transportation Modes Compared:**
  - **Road (Trucking):** Highest geographic accessibility, flexible door-to-door delivery, best for short-to-medium hauls, high carbon emissions.
  - **Rail:** Cost-effective for heavy, bulky, non-perishable commodities over long inland distances; inflexible schedules and fixed tracks.
  - **Water / Maritime:** Lowest freight cost per ton-kilometer; handles massive global container trade; slowest transit speeds.
  - **Air:** Highest speed and reliability; highest freight cost ($4\text{–}10\times$ road); suited for high-value, perishable, or emergency shipments.
  - **Pipeline:** Continuous one-way flow for crude oil, petroleum products, and natural gas; lowest variable operating costs; massive upfront fixed capital expenditure.
- **Load Consolidations:** Full Truckload (FTL - point-to-point dedicated) vs. Less-Than-Truckload (LTL - consolidated via hub-and-spoke terminals).

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Transport Mode | Relative Cost per Ton-km | Speed | Accessibility | Carbon Footprint |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Air** | Highest | Fastest | Low (airport to airport) | Highest |
| **Road (Truck)** | Moderate | Moderate | Highest (door-to-door) | Moderate |
| **Rail** | Low | Slow to Moderate | Moderate (rail siding needed) | Low |
| **Water** | Lowest | Slowest | Low (coastal / port bound) | Lowest |

## 4. Must Remember
- Transportation typically accounts for **$50\text{–}60\%$ of total corporate logistics expenditure**.
- The **Tapering Principle** explains why rate per kilometer is significantly cheaper on a 1,000 km haul than on a 100 km haul.
