# Week 10 – Lecture 2: Capacity Planning

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Capacity:** The maximum rate of output that an operational facility, warehouse, transport fleet, or worker team can achieve and sustain under specified operating conditions.
- **Design Capacity:** The theoretical maximum engineering output rate attainable under 100% ideal conditions with zero downtime, breaks, or maintenance.
- **Effective Capacity:** The realistic maximum output rate achievable taking into account scheduled preventive maintenance, product changeover setups, and normal operator rest allowances.

## 2. Important Points
- **System Utilization & Efficiency Metrics:**
  - **Utilization Rate:** Measures output against theoretical design limits:
    $$\text{Utilization} = \frac{\text{Actual Output}}{\text{Design Capacity}} \times 100\%$$
  - **Efficiency Rate:** Measures output against realistic operating expectations:
    $$\text{Efficiency} = \frac{\text{Actual Output}}{\text{Effective Capacity}} \times 100\%$$
- **Strategic Capacity Expansion Options:**
  - **Lead Strategy (Capacity Lead):** Adding capacity proactively ahead of anticipated demand growth; ensures high customer responsiveness and zero stockouts, but risks underutilized idle capital.
  - **Lag Strategy (Capacity Lag):** Adding capacity reactively only after customer demand has already materialized; ensures high utilization and minimal capital risk, but loses market share and creates long order backlogs.
  - **Match / Tracking Strategy:** Adding capacity in frequent small, modular increments to track actual demand growth closely.
- **Break-Even Volume in Capacity Decisions:**
  $$Q_{\text{BE}} = \frac{\text{Total Fixed Costs}}{\text{Selling Price per unit} - \text{Variable Cost per unit}}$$

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Capacity Expansion Strategy | Timing Relative to Demand | Capital Risk | Service Level Risk |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Lead Strategy** | Proactive (Ahead of demand) | High (idle capacity cost) | Lowest (never stock out) |
| **Lag Strategy** | Reactive (Behind demand) | Lowest (full utilization) | Highest (backlogs, lost sales) |
| **Match Strategy** | Incremental tracking | Moderate | Moderate |

## 4. Must Remember
- Design capacity is an unachievable theoretical ceiling; **Effective Capacity** is the only valid baseline for operational scheduling.
- Operating consistently above $90\text{–}95\%$ utilization creates exponential queueing delays, worker fatigue, machine breakdowns, and surging overtime costs.
