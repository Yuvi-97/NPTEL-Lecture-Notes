# Week 3 – Lecture 5: Lean Logistics (Contd.)

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Five Principles of Lean (Womack and Jones):** The foundational roadmap for transforming traditional push distribution into a high-velocity lean logistics enterprise.
- **Value Stream Mapping (VSM):** A visual diagnostic charting tool capturing the end-to-end flow of both physical goods and information signals from raw supplier to end customer.
- **Pull System & Kanban:** An execution mechanism where upstream logistics nodes only move and replenish stock in direct response to downstream customer consumption signals.

## 2. Important Points
- **The Five Core Principles Explained:**
  1. **Specify Value:** Clearly define what the customer values (e.g., guaranteed 24-hr delivery window, zero transit scuffs).
  2. **Map the Value Stream:** Map every step in the fulfillment pipeline and categorize steps into Value-Added (VA), Necessary Non-Value-Added (NNVA, e.g., customs paperwork), and Unnecessary Non-Value-Added (Pure Waste).
  3. **Create Flow:** Eliminate batch-and-queue waiting; establish smooth continuous flow from receiving to dispatch.
  4. **Establish Pull:** Replace speculative production forecasts with consumption-triggered Kanban pull signals.
  5. **Pursue Perfection:** Continually conduct Kaizen events to eradicate hidden waste and shorten order lead times.
- **Metrics in Value Stream Mapping:**
  - **Process Time / Cycle Time ($PT$):** Time actively spent working on the order.
  - **Lead Time ($LT$):** Total elapsed clock time from order placement to final delivery.
  - **Process Velocity / Efficiency:**
    $$\text{Process Efficiency} = \frac{\text{Total Value-Added Time}}{\text{Total Lead Time}} \times 100$$
    (In traditional logistics, process efficiency is frequently under $5\%$).

## 3. Differences / Examples
| System Model | Driving Mechanism | Inventory Stance | Flexibility |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Push System** | Production schedules based on long-range forecasts | High finished-goods buffer inventory | Low (vulnerable to demand shifts & obsolescence) |
| **Pull System (Lean)** | Downstream actual customer consumption (Kanban) | Minimal, visual buffer stock | High (responds rapidly to actual customer demand) |

## 4. Must Remember
- Reference text: **Womack & Jones**, *Lean Thinking: Banish Waste and Create Wealth in Your Corporation*.
- In typical unoptimized logistics networks, goods spend over **$90\text{–}95\%$ of their total lead time waiting idle** in storage bays, in transit yards, or in staging queues.
