# Week 3 – Lecture 4: Lean Logistics

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Lean Logistics:** A management philosophy derived from the Toyota Production System (TPS) focused on eliminating all forms of non-value-adding waste (*Muda*) while accelerating velocity and flow across the logistics pipeline.
- **Value-Added vs. Non-Value-Added Activities:**
  - *Value-Added (VA):* Any task the customer is actively willing to pay for that physically transforms or positions the product closer to final utility.
  - *Non-Value-Added (NVA / Waste):* Activities that consume labor, time, space, or capital without enhancing the customer's perceived product value.
- **Origins:** Pioneered by **Taiichi Ohno** and **Shigeo Shingo** at Toyota Motor Corporation.

## 2. Important Points
- **The "Three M's" of Japanese Lean Philosophy:**
  1. **Muda (Waste):** Operational activities consuming resources without generating customer value.
  2. **Muri (Overburden):** Pushing employees, vehicles, or warehouse racking beyond design capacity limits, leading to fatigue, accidents, and equipment breakdowns.
  3. **Mura (Unevenness / Inconsistency):** Erratic fluctuations in daily workloads, dispatch schedules, and delivery volumes.
- **The 7 Deadly Wastes (TIMWOOD) in Logistics:**
  - **T – Transportation:** Unnecessary multiple handoffs or double-handling of pallets.
  - **I – Inventory:** Holding excess safety stock tying up working capital and occupying aisle space.
  - **M – Motion:** Inefficient picker walking paths inside poorly laid out warehouses.
  - **W – Waiting:** Trucks queued at loading docks; pickers waiting for system paperwork.
  - **O – Overproduction:** Shipping goods ahead of real customer pull demand.
  - **O – Over-processing:** Excessive shrink-wrapping, duplicate label scanning, or unnecessary inspections.
  - **D – Defects:** Wrong picking, shipping to the wrong address, or transit damage requiring re-shipment.

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Lean Term | Japanese Meaning | Logistics Manifestation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Muda** | Waste | Storing obsolete, dead inventory in prime warehouse racks |
| **Muri** | Overburden | Loading a 10-ton truck with 14 tons of cargo, stressing the axle |
| **Mura** | Unevenness | Monday dock congestion followed by empty idle bays on Thursday |

## 4. Must Remember
- **Taiichi Ohno** identified inventory as the root of all wastes because high stock levels conceal operational bottlenecks, machine breakdowns, and supplier delays.
- Lean logistics emphasizes **smoothing flow** by systematically eliminating Mura, Muri, and Muda in unison.
