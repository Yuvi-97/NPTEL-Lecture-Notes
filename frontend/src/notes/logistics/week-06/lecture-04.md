# Week 6 – Lecture 4: Supply Chain Drivers (Logistical Drivers)

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Supply Chain Drivers:** The core structural components that determine whether a supply chain performs as cost-efficient, highly responsive, or a balanced hybrid.
- **The Six Drivers Framework (Chopra & Meindl):** Categorized into **Three Logistical Drivers** (Facilities, Inventory, Transportation) and **Three Cross-Functional Drivers** (Information, Sourcing, Pricing).
- **Fundamental Trade-off:** Increasing responsiveness generally inflates operational costs; optimizing drivers aligns the chain with its target competitive strategy.

## 2. Important Points
- **The Three Logistical Drivers Detailed:**
  1. **Facilities:**
     - The physical locations where inventory is stored, fabricated, or cross-docked.
     - *Role:* Centralized facilities achieve high economies of scale and low operating costs (efficient); decentralized regional facilities locate close to customers, shortening delivery lead times (responsive).
     - *Key Decisions:* Location, capacity sizing, manufacturing flexibility (flexible vs. dedicated lines).
  2. **Inventory:**
     - All raw materials, work-in-process (WIP), and finished goods held within the supply chain.
     - *Role:* Holding large inventories buffers against stockouts and supports short lead times (responsive); lean inventories minimize carrying costs (efficient).
     - *Components:* Cycle inventory (batch sizing), Safety inventory (uncertainty buffer), Seasonal inventory (leveling production).
  3. **Transportation:**
     - Moving products between facilities across the supply chain stages.
     - *Role:* Fast transportation modes (air, dedicated express couriers) maximize responsiveness; slower, bulk modes (ocean shipping, unit trains) maximize cost efficiency.
     - *Trade-off:* High freight spend vs. lower inventory carrying cost (faster transit eliminates pipeline inventory).

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Driver | Efficiency Strategy (Low Cost) | Responsiveness Strategy (High Speed) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Facilities** | Few centralized hubs; high capacity utilization | Multiple localized micro-hubs; excess capacity buffer |
| **Inventory** | Low inventory; high turns; risk of stockout tolerated | High safety stock; wide variety; stockouts strictly avoided |
| **Transportation** | Slow, bulk shipping (ocean, rail, full truckloads) | Fast, frequent shipments (air freight, express couriers) |

## 4. Must Remember
- Reference text: **Chopra & Meindl**, *Supply Chain Management*.
- A company cannot achieve responsiveness simply by demanding it: the physical configuration of its **facilities, inventory buffers, and transport modes** must support that speed.
