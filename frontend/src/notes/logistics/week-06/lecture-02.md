# Week 6 – Lecture 2: Supply Chain Operational Views

## 1. Key Concepts
- **SCOR Model (Supply Chain Operations Reference):** A standardized cross-industry diagnostic framework developed by the Supply Chain Council (now APICS/ASCM) defining six core management processes: **Plan**, **Source**, **Make**, **Deliver**, **Return**, and **Enable**.
- **Cycle View of Supply Chain:** Decomposes supply chain operations into a series of interconnected cycles, each executing at the physical interface between two successive supply chain stages.
- **Push / Pull View of Supply Chain:** Categorizes all processes based on whether they are initiated in anticipation of future customer orders (**Push**) or in direct response to an actual confirmed customer order (**Pull**).

## 2. Important Points
- **The Four Process Cycles in the Cycle View:**
  1. *Customer Order Cycle:* Customer $\longleftrightarrow$ Retailer (order arrival, picking, delivery, and receipt).
  2. *Replenishment Cycle:* Retailer $\longleftrightarrow$ Distributor (inventory replenishment, bulk order generation).
  3. *Manufacturing Cycle:* Distributor $\longleftrightarrow$ Manufacturer (production scheduling, assembly, and dispatch).
  4. *Procurement Cycle:* Manufacturer $\longleftrightarrow$ Tier-1 Supplier (raw material purchasing, component shipping).
- **The Push/Pull Boundary (Order Decoupling Point):**
  - **Push Processes:** Executed under high demand uncertainty based on sales forecasts; risk of excess stock or stockouts.
  - **Pull Processes:** Executed under zero demand uncertainty after order commitment; risk of execution delay.
  - The push/pull boundary defines where speculative manufacturing transitions into responsive order fulfillment.

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Operational Perspective | Core Analytical Focus | Primary Benefit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cycle View** | Stage-to-stage transactional interfaces | Clarifies precise responsibilities and roles between adjacent partners |
| **Push / Pull View** | Timing relative to customer order | Dictates aggregate strategic postponement and inventory buffer positioning |

## 4. Must Remember
- The **SCOR model** establishes standardized KPIs across 5 performance attributes: Reliability, Responsiveness, Agility, Costs, and Asset Management.
- Moving the push/pull boundary upstream makes the chain more forecast-driven (efficient); moving it downstream makes the chain more order-driven (responsive).
