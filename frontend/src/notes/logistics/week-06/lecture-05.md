# Week 6 – Lecture 5: Supply Chain Drivers (Cross-Functional Drivers)

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Cross-Functional Drivers:** The organizational, contractual, and informational levers that connect, coordinate, and govern physical logistical assets across the entire enterprise.
- **The Three Cross-Functional Drivers (Chopra & Meindl):** **Information**, **Sourcing**, and **Pricing**.
- **The Information Multiplier:** Information acts as the master connector: accurate, real-time data allows supply chains to replace expensive physical inventory buffers with digital visibility.

## 2. Important Points
- **The Three Cross-Functional Drivers Detailed:**
  1. **Information:**
     - Data and analytics regarding facilities, inventory, transportation, costs, prices, and customers.
     - *Role:* Enables synchronization, forecasting, and exception handling across partners.
     - *Technologies:* Electronic Data Interchange (EDI), ERP systems, Point-of-Sale (POS) data sharing, and RFID.
     - *Trade-off:* High software investment vs. superior coordination and waste elimination.
  2. **Sourcing:**
     - The business decisions of which business tasks will be performed in-house and which outsourced to third parties.
     - *Role:* Identifies world-class external supplier capabilities; manages supplier portfolios, contracts, and procurement.
     - *Trade-off:* Retaining in-house control vs. accessing lower external supplier costs and specialized innovation.
  3. **Pricing:**
     - How much a company charges for products, delivery tiers, and return services.
     - *Role:* Balances supply with demand; smooths operational load through differential pricing (e.g., peak vs. off-peak delivery charges, volume discounts).
     - *Strategies:* Everyday Low Pricing (EDLP) stabilizes demand patterns; High-Low promotional pricing induces artificial demand spikes.

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Cross-Functional Driver | Primary Decision Scope | Impact on Supply Chain Performance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Information** | Data infrastructure, POS sharing, demand sensing | Eliminates Bullwhip distortion; replaces safety stock with data |
| **Sourcing** | Make vs. Buy, vendor selection, contract terms | Dictates cost structure, supplier risk, and production quality |
| **Pricing** | Volume discounts, dynamic surge pricing, EDLP | Smooths seasonal demand peaks; aligns customer incentives |

## 4. Must Remember
- **Information is the foundation of modern SCM:** without transparent information sharing, inventory buffers must artificially expand at every echelon.
- Everyday Low Pricing (EDLP) is preferred in efficient supply chains because temporary promotional price cuts trigger disruptive forward-buying and bullwhip oscillations.
