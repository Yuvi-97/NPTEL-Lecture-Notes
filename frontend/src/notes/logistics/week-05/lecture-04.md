# Week 5 – Lecture 4: Principle Streams of Supply Chain (Internal & Downstream)

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Internal Conversion Stream:** The sequence of factory shop-floor operations that transforms raw materials and sub-assemblies into finished, salable products.
- **Downstream Distribution Stream:** The outbound network of fulfillment centers, cross-docks, transport modes, and retail channels that delivers finished products to the ultimate consumer.
- **Customer Order Decoupling Point (CODP) / Order Penetration Point:** The precise location in the supply chain where an actual customer order enters the system, separating forecast-driven push activities from order-driven pull execution.

## 2. Important Points
- **Manufacturing Operating Environments (based on CODP):**
  - **Make-to-Stock (MTS):** Products are manufactured based entirely on sales forecasts and stored in finished goods warehouses (CODP at final goods inventory; shortest customer delivery lead time).
  - **Assemble-to-Order (ATO):** Standard modular components are pre-manufactured to forecast, but final assembly occurs only after receiving a specific customer order (CODP at sub-assembly stage; e.g., custom laptops).
  - **Make-to-Order (MTO):** Production starts only after receiving an authorized customer order; raw materials are pre-stocked (CODP at raw materials).
  - **Engineer-to-Order (ETO):** Highly customized engineering design and fabrication initiated entirely upon customer specification (CODP at design stage; longest lead time; e.g., large turbines, bridges).
- **Downstream Channel Architecture:**
  - *Direct-to-Consumer (D2C):* Manufacturer ships directly to end user (high margin, complex parcel fulfillment).
  - *Multi-Tier Intermediated Distribution:* Manufacturer $\to$ Central DC $\to$ Regional Wholesaler $\to$ Retailer $\to$ Customer (efficient bulk consolidation, shared inventory holding).

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Manufacturing Strategy | Customer Lead Time | Inventory Held | Product Customization |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Make-to-Stock (MTS)** | Very Short (off-the-shelf) | High finished goods inventory | Low (standardized commodities) |
| **Assemble-to-Order (ATO)**| Moderate (hours to days) | Sub-assemblies and modular parts | Moderate (modular options) |
| **Make-to-Order (MTO)** | Long (weeks) | Raw materials only | High (custom specifications) |

## 4. Must Remember
- Upstream of the **CODP**, operations are driven by speculative **push forecasts**; downstream of the CODP, operations are driven by actual **customer pull demand**.
- Postponement strategies push the CODP as close to the final customer as possible, minimizing inventory holding costs and obsolescence.
