# Week 5 – Lecture 1: Fundamentals of Supply Chain Management

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Supply Chain Management (SCM):** The systemic, strategic coordination of business functions across the entire extended enterprise—from raw material suppliers to final end customers—aimed at maximizing long-term competitive advantage and supply chain profitability.
- **Supply Chain Surplus:** The total value generated by a supply chain, defined as the revenue received from the final customer minus the total collective costs incurred across all stages of the chain:
  $$\text{Supply Chain Surplus} = \text{Customer Revenue} - \text{Total Costs Across All Stages}$$
- **Focal Firm:** The leading organization (e.g., brand owner or original equipment manufacturer) that orchestrates the supply chain network and directly interfaces with the primary market.

## 2. Important Points
- **The Three Decision Phases in SCM:**
  1. **Supply Chain Strategy / Design (Long-Term: Multi-Year):** Decisions regarding plant locations, warehouse network configuration, facility capacity sizing, technology investments, and in-house vs. outsourcing policies.
  2. **Supply Chain Planning (Medium-Term: Monthly to Annual):** Demand forecasting, aggregate production planning, inventory stocking policies, promotional timing, and seasonal subcontracting.
  3. **Supply Chain Operation (Short-Term: Daily to Weekly):** Real-time customer order processing, individual warehouse picking assignments, delivery truck routing, and shipping schedule fulfillment.
- **Key Supply Chain Stakeholders:** Tier-2 suppliers (raw materials), Tier-1 suppliers (sub-assemblies), Focal Manufacturer, Wholesalers/Distributors, Retailers, End Consumers, and 3PL logistics providers.

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Decision Phase | Time Horizon | Primary Focus | Flexibility |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Strategy / Design** | Multi-year ($3\text{–}10\text{ yrs}$) | Facility locations, core competencies, network footprint | Fixed / Very expensive to alter |
| **Planning** | Quarter to Year ($3\text{–}12\text{ mos}$) | Inventory targets, aggregate capacity, subcontracting | Moderate flexibility within fixed assets |
| **Operation** | Hours to Days | Order dispatching, truck loading, dock allocations | High / Fast tactical adjustments |

## 4. Must Remember
- The ultimate source of revenue for any supply chain is the **end consumer**: money flowing between intermediate partners is merely internal fund transfers.
- Supply chain success is measured by total **Supply Chain Surplus**, not by whether an individual partner maximizes its isolated profit at the expense of others.
