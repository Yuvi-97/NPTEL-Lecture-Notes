# Week 5 – Lecture 2: SC Operations: Manufacturing vs. Service Supply Chains

## 1. Key Concepts
- **Manufacturing Supply Chain:** A tangible, product-oriented network focused on physical material procurement, factory conversion, warehousing, and transportation of physical goods.
- **Service Supply Chain:** A process-oriented network managing the flow of intangible services, information, human talent, and customer experiences (e.g., healthcare, banking, hospitality, software).
- **The IHIP Framework of Services:** Four distinguishing characteristics: **Intangibility**, **Heterogeneity** (high variation in human execution), **Inseparability** (production and consumption occur simultaneously), and **Perishability** (cannot be stored in inventory).

## 2. Important Points
- **Customer as Co-Producer:** In service supply chains, the customer is physically or digitally present inside the production process (e.g., a patient in a hospital, a passenger on a flight), providing real-time inputs.
- **Inventory & Capacity Constraints:**
  - *Manufacturing:* Demand volatility is buffered by physical inventory (safety stock, finished goods warehouses).
  - *Services:* Service capacity is strictly perishable; an unsold airline seat, vacant hotel room, or idle doctor hour represents permanent, unrecoverable revenue loss.
- **Capacity Management Strategies in Services:**
  - Dynamic surge pricing / peak-load yield management to shift customer demand.
  - Queue management systems and multi-skilled cross-trained staff.
  - Customer self-service portals (e.g., mobile banking, self-checkout kiosks).

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Dimension | Manufacturing Supply Chain | Service Supply Chain |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Output Nature** | Tangible, standardized physical goods | Intangible, customized service experiences |
| **Inventory Role** | Buffers demand uncertainty via stock | Cannot be stored; buffer capacity is labor/time |
| **Customer Role** | Passive recipient at the end of the chain | Active co-producer present during execution |
| **Lead Time Driver** | Production runs, transit distance, freight | Waiting queues, human service interaction time |

## 4. Must Remember
- Because services cannot build physical inventory buffers, service supply chain efficiency hinges entirely on **real-time capacity and queue management**.
- Dual directionality: Service supply chains require bi-directional information exchanges where customer feedback directly alters immediate service delivery.
