# Week 5 – Lecture 3: Principle Streams of Supply Chain (Upstream)

## 1. Key Concepts
- **The Three Core Supply Chain Streams:**
  1. *Upstream / Inbound Stream:* Sourcing, procurement, and supplier relationship management.
  2. *Internal Stream:* Manufacturing conversion, WIP scheduling, and assembly.
  3. *Downstream / Outbound Stream:* Distribution, warehousing, order fulfillment, and customer delivery.
- **Strategic Sourcing:** The systematic business process of evaluating, selecting, and collaborating with external vendors to align supply capabilities with corporate competitive objectives.
- **Total Cost of Ownership (TCO):** A comprehensive financial estimate capturing all direct and indirect expenses incurred throughout an asset's lifecycle, far beyond the initial quoted purchase price.

## 2. Important Points
- **Elements of Total Cost of Ownership (TCO):**
  - *Acquisition Costs:* Purchase price, currency exchange rates, contracting expenses.
  - *Operational / Logistics Costs:* Freight transport, customs duties, incoming inspection, material handling.
  - *Possession / Inventory Costs:* Warehouse holding costs, capital financing charges, taxes, insurance, risk of obsolescence.
  - *Quality & Non-Performance Costs:* Rework, defective parts scrap, late delivery penalties, plant downtime costs.
- **Sourcing Strategy Decisions:**
  - **Single Sourcing:** Partnering with one dedicated vendor per component; maximizes volume economies of scale, dedicated engineering collaboration, and shared quality systems (higher dependency risk).
  - **Multiple Sourcing:** Distributing purchase volumes across several competing suppliers; fosters competitive bidding and provides backup capacity during supplier disruptions.

## 3. Differences / Examples
| Sourcing Approach | Primary Advantages | Major Risks / Drawbacks |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Single Sourcing** | Maximum volume discounts, close trust, shared innovation | Vulnerable to single point of failure (supplier strike, fire) |
| **Multiple Sourcing** | High supply assurance, competitive pricing leverage | Fragmented volume discounts, higher administrative overhead |

## 4. Must Remember
- Purchasing is tactical (buying at the lowest invoice price); **Strategic Sourcing** is holistic (optimizing Total Cost of Ownership and long-term supplier capabilities).
- Evaluating suppliers solely on unit purchase price frequently results in higher overall costs due to poor quality, scrap rates, and delivery delays.
